# CNS Space-Occupying Lesions - Management Protocol

## Overview

Comprehensive guideline for the assessment and treatment of cerebral space-occupying lesions in oncology and haematology patients, covering both primary and metastatic brain tumors.

**Source**: Cambridge University Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust (Guideline OH/C048, May 2018)

---

## Imaging Protocols

### Primary Brain Tumors

**Minimum MRI requirements:**
- **T1-weighted** imaging
- **T2-weighted** imaging
- **T1 with gadolinium** (contrast-enhanced)
- **DWI (Diffusion-Weighted Imaging)**: Essential to exclude abscesses

### Metastatic Brain Tumors

**Prior to Neuroscience SMDT opinion:**
- MRI brain with T1, T2, and T1 + gadolinium

**Suspected metastases:**
- **CXR** OR **CT chest, abdomen, and pelvis**
- Rule out primary malignancy

**Confirmed metastases:**
- **CT chest, abdomen, and pelvis** (mandatory)
- **Restaging** if known primary disease

### Key Imaging Principle

> **DWI is critical**: Reduces risk of missing cerebral abscesses, which can mimic tumors

---

## Initial Investigations

### Essential Blood Tests

**All patients:**
- FBC
- U&E / Creatinine
- LFTs
- Clotting screen

**Suspected metastases (additional):**
- Relevant **tumor markers**
- **Calcium** (hypercalcemia of malignancy)

## Medical Management

### Steroids (Dexamethasone)

#### Indications
**Start Dexamethasone 8mg BD** in all patients EXCEPT suspected lymphoma

#### Mechanism of Action
- **Reduces ICP** by minimizing peritumoral edema
- **Rapidly resolves symptoms**
- **Improves appetite**
- **Anti-emetic** effect

#### Pharmacokinetics
- **Half-life**: 2 days
- **Steady state**: Takes days to achieve
- **Timing**: Give early in day (disturbs sleep if given late)

#### Duration Considerations
- **<2-3 weeks**: Can stop without weaning
- **>2-3 weeks**: Suppresses adrenal function → **wean carefully**

#### Dosing Strategy
> Use **minimum dose** required to control symptoms

#### Special Consideration: Lymphoma
⚠️ **AVOID dexamethasone** pre-operatively in suspected lymphoma
- Steroids can affect histological diagnosis
- May cause tumor lysis
- Discuss with neurosurgeon if patient rapidly deteriorating (low-dose may be given)

---

### Gastric Protection

#### Proton Pump Inhibitors
**Omeprazole** (or alternative PPI) mandatory while on steroids

**Rationale**: Protect stomach lining from steroid-induced ulceration

#### Oral Candidiasis
May develop with steroid use:
- **Nystatin 1ml QDS**, OR
- **Fluconazole** (course)

---

### Analgesia

#### Stepwise Approach

**First-line:**
- **Paracetamol** - safe and effective
- Can use alone or in combination

**Second-line (Opioids):**
- **Codeine**: Traditionally used (allegedly less sedative - unproven)
- **Tramadol**: Acceptable alternative
- **Morphine**: Acceptable alternative

#### NSAIDs

**Effective analgesics** - widely used BUT:

**Concerns:**
- ⚠️ Effect on blood clotting
- ⚠️ May impair wound healing
- ⚠️ **In combination with steroids** = significant risk of **gastric ulceration**

**Use cautiously** and ensure adequate PPI cover

---

### Antiplatelet Agents

#### Stop Before Surgery

**Aspirin and Clopidogrel:**
- **Stop immediately** if surgery planned
- **No surgery for 10 days** after even a single dose
- Essential for surgical hemostasis

---

### Anti-Epileptic Drugs

#### Background
- **Many patients** with intracranial tumors develop epilepsy
- May have major seizures or focal attacks
- **Change in seizure character** may indicate tumor transformation

#### Prophylaxis
**Routine prophylaxis**: Unproven benefit

**After single seizure**: Reasonable to start therapy given:
- Structural abnormality present
- Continuing liability to epilepsy

#### First-Line: Carbamazepine

**Preparation**: Carbamazepine Retard (slow-release)

**Dosing:**
1. **Start**: 100 mg BD
2. **After 2 weeks**: Increase to 200 mg BD
3. **Check blood level** and adjust dose to therapeutic range

#### Second-Line Options

**If Carbamazepine poorly tolerated:**
- **Lamotrigine**
- **Sodium Valproate**
  - Note: Valproate blood levels are of **limited clinical value**

**If compliance is an issue:**
- **Phenytoin** (once daily dosing)
  - Start: 200 mg per day
  - Check blood levels after 7-10 days and adjust

#### Add-On Therapy

**If seizures continue despite first-line drug:**

**Levetiracetam:**
- **Start**: 250 mg BD
- **Titrate** according to response and tolerance
- **Maximum**: 1500 mg BD

## Clinical Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Known Cancer Patient with New Neurological Symptoms

**Immediate actions:**
1. Clinical assessment + neurological examination
2. **MRI brain** with T1, T2, T1+gadolinium
3. **Dexamethasone 8mg BD** + PPI
4. Stop antiplatelet agents
5. **CT chest/abdomen/pelvis** for staging
6. Bloods: FBC, U&E, LFT, clotting, calcium, tumor markers

### Scenario 2: First Seizure with Suspected SOL

**Immediate actions:**
1. Stabilize patient (ABCs)
2. **CT or MRI brain**
3. **Do NOT give dexamethasone** if lymphoma suspected
4. Blood tests as above
5. Consider starting **carbamazepine** if structural lesion confirmed

### Scenario 3: Rapidly Deteriorating Patient with Brain Tumor

**Immediate actions:**
1. **ABC assessment**
2. Consider **low-dose dexamethasone** (discuss with neurosurgeon first)
3. Urgent imaging if not already done
4. **Urgent neurosurgical referral**
5. May need emergency decompression or CSF diversion

## Key Management Principles

### 1. Differentiate Primary vs Metastatic
- **Metastatic**: Look for primary source (CT CAP)
- **Primary**: MRI with DWI to exclude abscess

### 2. Steroid Management
- Start early (except lymphoma)
- Use minimum effective dose
- Always prescribe PPI
- Wean carefully if >2-3 weeks duration

### 3. Seizure Management
- Not routine prophylaxis
- Treat after first seizure (structural abnormality present)
- First-line: Carbamazepine
- Monitor drug levels

### 4. Surgical Preparation
- Stop aspirin/clopidogrel (10 days before surgery)
- Check coagulation
- Optimize steroid dose

### 5. MDT Approach
- All cases discussed at neurosurgical MDT
- Input from neurosurgery, oncology, radiology
- Treatment plan: surgery, radiotherapy, chemotherapy, or palliative

---

## Complications to Monitor

### Steroid-Related
- **Hyperglycemia** (diabetes)
- **Gastric ulceration** (especially with NSAIDs)
- **Oral candidiasis**
- **Myopathy** (chronic use)
- **Psychiatric effects** (mood changes, psychosis)
- **Immunosuppression**
- **Adrenal suppression** (>2-3 weeks)

### Tumor-Related
- **Raised ICP** (headache, vomiting, drowsiness, papilledema)
- **Seizures** (focal or generalized)
- **Focal neurological deficits**
- **Hydrocephalus** (obstructive or communicating)
- **Hemorrhage into tumor**
- **Edema** (vasogenic)

### Post-Surgical
- **Hemorrhage**
- **Infection** (wound, meningitis, abscess)
- **CSF leak**
- **Edema** (peri-operative swelling)
- **Seizures**

## Treatment Modalities

### Surgery
**Indications:**
- Symptomatic mass effect
- Diagnostic uncertainty (biopsy)
- Solitary metastasis (good prognosis)
- Hydrocephalus (shunt/EVD)

**Goals:**
- Maximal safe resection
- Tissue diagnosis
- Decompress mass effect
- Improve quality of life

### Radiotherapy
**Whole Brain Radiotherapy (WBRT):**
- Multiple brain metastases
- Adjuvant to surgery

**Stereotactic Radiosurgery (SRS):**
- Small metastases (<3cm)
- Limited number of lesions (1-4)
- Radioresistant tumors

### Chemotherapy
**Primary tumors:**
- Glioblastoma: Temozolomide
- Lymphoma: Methotrexate-based

**Metastases:**
- Depends on primary tumor type
- Small cell lung, breast often responsive

## Interview Key Points

### Dexamethasone Mechanism
- **Reduces vasogenic edema** around tumor
- Restores blood-brain barrier integrity
- Half-life 2 days (slow onset, slow offset)
- Must wean if used >2-3 weeks (HPA axis suppression)

### Why Avoid Steroids in Lymphoma?
- Lymphomas are **steroid-sensitive**
- Steroids can cause rapid tumor lysis
- May **obscure histological diagnosis**
- Makes tissue diagnosis difficult/impossible

### First-Line AED for Tumor-Related Epilepsy
- Traditionally **carbamazepine** (retard preparation)
- Increasingly **levetiracetam** used (fewer drug interactions)
- Prophylaxis NOT routinely recommended (unproven benefit)

### Investigation of Solitary Brain Lesion
1. **MRI with gadolinium** (essential)
2. **DWI sequence** (exclude abscess)
3. **CT chest/abdomen/pelvis** (look for primary)
4. Consider other investigations:
   - CSF analysis if leptomeningeal disease suspected
   - Whole-body PET-CT for occult primary
   - Biopsy if diagnosis uncertain

## Common Interview Questions

**Q: A 65-year-old with known lung cancer presents with headache and confusion. What is your management?**

A:
1. **Clinical assessment**: GCS, focal neurology, signs of raised ICP
2. **Imaging**: Urgent MRI brain with gadolinium (or CT if MRI unavailable)
3. **Immediate treatment**:
   - Dexamethasone 8mg BD + PPI
   - Stop antiplatelet agents
4. **Investigations**: Bloods (FBC, U&E, LFT, clotting, calcium), restaging CT CAP
5. **Referral**: Neurosurgical MDT discussion
6. **Treatment options**: Surgery (if solitary + good performance status), radiotherapy (WBRT or SRS), systemic therapy

**Q: Why do we give PPI with dexamethasone?**

A: Dexamethasone increases risk of gastric ulceration. Risk is significantly increased when combined with NSAIDs. PPI (e.g., omeprazole) provides gastric protection.

**Q: What are the indications for surgery in brain metastases?**

A:
- **Solitary metastasis** with good performance status and controlled systemic disease
- **Symptomatic mass effect** despite steroids
- **Diagnostic uncertainty** (biopsy)
- **Radioresistant tumor** (melanoma, renal cell)
- **Posterior fossa lesions** with hydrocephalus

**Q: How do you differentiate abscess from tumor on imaging?**

A:
- **DWI (Diffusion-Weighted Imaging)**: Abscesses show **restricted diffusion** (bright on DWI, dark on ADC)
- Tumors typically show facilitated diffusion
- **MR spectroscopy**: Abscess shows lactate, acetate, amino acids
- Clinical context: Fever, raised inflammatory markers suggest abscess

**Q: When would you start anti-epileptic drugs in a patient with brain tumor?**

A:
- **NOT routinely** as prophylaxis (unproven benefit)
- **After first seizure** (structural abnormality + ongoing epilepsy risk)
- **Perioperatively** (if cortical surgery/eloquent areas)
- First-line: Carbamazepine retard or levetiracetam

**Q: Why do we stop antiplatelet agents before neurosurgery?**

A:
- Aspirin/clopidogrel impair platelet function
- Increase risk of **intra-operative hemorrhage**
- Affect **post-operative hemostasis**
- Must stop **10 days** before surgery (even after single dose)

**Q: How long does dexamethasone take to work?**

A:
- **Half-life**: 2 days
- Takes **several days** to reach steady state
- Symptom improvement usually within **24-48 hours**
- Important: Give early in day (disturbs sleep if late dosing)

**Q: What is the risk of prolonged steroid use?**

A:
- **<2-3 weeks**: Can stop without weaning
- **>2-3 weeks**: HPA axis suppression - must wean carefully
- Complications: Hyperglycemia, myopathy, psychiatric effects, immunosuppression, gastric ulceration, oral candidiasis

## Practical Tips for Interviews

### Key Examination Findings
- **Papilledema**: Raised ICP
- **Focal neurological deficit**: Localizing lesion
- **Seizures**: Cortical involvement
- **Cognitive impairment**: Frontal or diffuse disease
- **Personality change**: Frontal lobe tumors

### Red Flags
- **Rapid deterioration**: Consider hemorrhage, hydrocephalus, herniation
- **Morning headache with vomiting**: Classic raised ICP
- **New-onset seizures in adult**: Brain tumor until proven otherwise
- **Progressive neurological deficit**: Mass effect

### Decision-Making Framework
1. **Is this primary or metastatic?** (Age, history, imaging appearance)
2. **Is the patient symptomatic?** (Steroids, surgery)
3. **Is there mass effect?** (Urgent decompression)
4. **What is the prognosis?** (Performance status, systemic disease control)
5. **What are the treatment options?** (Surgery, radiotherapy, chemotherapy)
6. **MDT discussion mandatory** for treatment planning
