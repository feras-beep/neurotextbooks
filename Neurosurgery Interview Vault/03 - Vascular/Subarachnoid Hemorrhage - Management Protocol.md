# Subarachnoid Hemorrhage - Management Protocol

## Overview
Non-traumatic SAH requires meticulous ward-based management to prevent complications including vasospasm, re-bleeding, hydrocephalus, seizures, and hyponatraemia.

**Source**: Cambridge University Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust (Version 2, February 2018)

---

## Pre-Treatment Management (Pre-Coiling/Pre-Clipping)

### Monitoring
- **Neurological observations**: Hourly
- **Bed rest**: Limited head elevation, commode use only
- **Oxygen**: Maintain SpO2 >96% with supplemental O2 as needed
- **Escort**: Competent staff member for transfers (CT/Angiography)

### Fluid Management
- **3 litres IV normal saline 0.9% per 24 hours**
  - ⚠️ Caution in elderly or cardiac patients
  - Aim for **euvolaemia**
  - **NEVER** use dextrose saline or gelofusin on ward

### Key Medications

#### Nimodipine
- **Standard dose**: 60 mg PO every 4 hours
- **Low BP dose**: 30 mg every 2 hours if SBP <90 mmHg
- **Duration**: 21 days from ictus
- **Evidence**: Reduces cerebral infarction (British Aneurysm Nimodipine Trial, BMJ 1989)

#### Analgesia
- **IV Paracetamol**: 1g TDS
- **Codeine**: 30-60mg PO QDS
- **❌ AVOID NSAIDs**: Impair hemostasis

### Blood Pressure Management

**Key Principle**: Balance competing risks of re-bleeding (hypertension) vs infarction (hypotension)

**Target BP**:
- SBP: 90-160 mmHg
- MAP: 105-120 mmHg

**Important**: Avoid sharp drops in BP

**Rationale**: Post-SAH, cerebral autoregulation is compromised → brain perfusion becomes more BP-dependent

**Action if SBP <90 mmHg**: Consider nimodipine 30 mg every 2 hours

### Metabolic Control
- **Glucose**: 6-10 mmol/L
  - Hyperglycemia associated with worse outcomes
  - Both hyper- and hypoglycemia should be avoided

### Laboratory Monitoring
**On admission**:
- FBC
- U&Es
- Coagulation
- Group & Screen
- Cross-match (if for theatre)

**Daily**: U&Es (watch sodium closely)

### Other Measures
- **Laxatives**: Lactulose 10 mg BD (prevent straining)
- **VTE prophylaxis**: Intermittent pneumatic compression devices
  - LMWH only started 24 hours post-procedure

## Post-Treatment Management (Post-Coiling/Post-Clipping)

### Early Post-Operative Care
- **Day 1**: Patient can sit out in chair
- **Mobility**: Gradual return to no restrictions
- **LMWH**: Start 24 hours post-procedure (dalteparin)

### Blood Pressure Management
- **More permissive**: Avoid aggressive BP control
- **Action required only if**:
  - SBP >200 mmHg, OR
  - MAP >130 mmHg

### Continued Monitoring
- **U&Es**: Daily (sodium monitoring critical)
- **Strict fluid balance**: Continue input/output monitoring
- **Wound care**: Clips/sutures removed day 5

### Discharge Planning
- **Optimum length of stay**: 6-8 days
- **Do NOT discharge if**: Severe headache (vasospasm risk)
- **Follow-up**: Clinic in 3 months

## Complications - Recognition & Management

### 1. Vasospasm (46% incidence)

#### Clinical Features
- Decreasing or fluctuating level of consciousness
- Focal neurological signs (stroke-like symptoms)
- Increasing headache

#### Management
1. **ABCs**
2. **IV fluid bolus**
3. **Aim to increase MAP** (hypertensive therapy)
4. **CT perfusion scan**
5. **Discuss with SpR and NCCU**

**Peak time**: Days 4-14 post-ictus

---

### 2. Re-bleeding (7% incidence)

#### Clinical Features
- Sudden reduction in GCS
- Headache increasing in severity
- Periods of raised BP

#### Management
1. **ABCs**
2. **Urgent CT head**
3. **Discuss with SpR and NCCU**

**Highest risk**: First 24-72 hours before aneurysm secured

---

### 3. Hydrocephalus (20% incidence)

#### Clinical Features
- Gradual decline in GCS
- Increasing headache
- Nausea and vomiting

#### Management
1. **ABCs**
2. **CT head**
3. **Surgical intervention**
   - **EVD** if blood in 3rd/4th ventricles
   - **Lumbar drain** if no blood in 3rd/4th ventricles

---

### 4. Seizure (3-22% incidence)

#### Management
1. **ABCs**
2. **Check U&E**: Sodium level
3. **CT head**: Exclude alternative cause
4. **Loading dose**: IV Levetiracetam 1g
5. **Maintenance**: PO Levetiracetam 500 mg BD

---

### 5. Hyponatraemia (30-56% incidence)

#### Differential Diagnosis

**Cerebral Salt Wasting**
- **Volume status**: Hypovolaemic
- **Urine output**: Polyuria
- **Management**:
  - IV sodium chloride 0.9%
  - Aim for euvolaemia

**SIADH**
- **Volume status**: Euvolaemic
- **Urine output**: Oliguria
- **Management**:
  - Fluid restriction
  - Titrate to sodium level

#### Clinical Importance
Monitor sodium daily - critical for preventing neurological deterioration

## Clinical Pathway Timeline

### Day 1 (Admission/Pre-op)
- Hourly neuro obs
- Bed rest
- 3L IV normal saline
- Start nimodipine
- Daily bloods (U&E, FBC)
- BP management
- Clip/coil within 72 hours if clinically well

### Post-Operation
- Read op notes for post-op instructions
- Start LMWH 24 hours post-procedure
- Continue nimodipine (21-day course)
- Antiplatelet therapy if endovascular treatment

### Days 2-6 (Ongoing Care)
- Monitor for complications (especially vasospasm)
- Observe for hypo/hypernatraemia
- Progress mobility
- Strict fluid balance
- Daily U&Es

### Days 6-8 (Discharge)
- Remove clips/sutures day 5
- Ensure headache not severe
- Discharge information and wound care leaflet
- 7 days medication supply
- Contact details (CNS)
- Follow-up in 3 months

## Interview Key Points

### Management Principles
1. **Secure aneurysm early** (within 72 hours)
2. **Prevent re-bleeding** (BP control, bed rest)
3. **Prevent vasospasm** (nimodipine, euvolaemia)
4. **Recognize complications early** (hourly neuro obs)
5. **Avoid secondary insults** (hypotension, hypoxia, hyponatraemia)

### Common Interview Questions

**Q: Why do we give nimodipine?**
A: Calcium channel blocker that reduces cerebral infarction and improves outcomes post-SAH. Given for 21 days from ictus.

**Q: How do you differentiate cerebral salt wasting from SIADH?**
A: Both cause hyponatraemia, but:
- **CSW**: Hypovolaemic with polyuria → treat with IV saline
- **SIADH**: Euvolaemic with oliguria → treat with fluid restriction

**Q: What is the most common complication of SAH?**
A: Vasospasm (46%), typically occurs days 4-14 post-ictus

**Q: Why do we avoid NSAIDs in SAH?**
A: They impair hemostasis and may increase bleeding risk

**Q: What BP targets do you use pre-treatment?**
A: SBP 90-160 mmHg or MAP 105-120 mmHg to balance re-bleed vs infarction risk
