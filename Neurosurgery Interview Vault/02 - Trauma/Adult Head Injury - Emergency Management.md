# Adult Head Injury - Emergency Management

## Classification by GCS

**Mild**: GCS 13-15
**Moderate**: GCS 9-12
**Severe**: GCS 3-8

Classification determined after primary survey resuscitation.

---

## Severe & Moderate Head Injury (GCS 3-12)

### Initial Management

**Location**: Resuscitation room
**Approach**: ATLS principles - ABCs first
**Team**: Trauma team activation

### Immediate Assessment
- **Neurological exam** (mini neuro exam):
  - Glasgow Coma Scale
  - Pupil examination
  - Gross neurological deficit

### Investigations
- **Urgent CT head and neck**
- **Neurosurgical referral** mandatory

### Neuroprotective Measures

**Critical for ALL ventilated head injury patients:**

| Parameter | Target |
|-----------|--------|
| **Blood Pressure** | >100 mmHg (50-69 years)<br>>110 mmHg (15-49 & >70 years) |
| **Oxygenation** | Normal PaO₂ |
| **PaCO₂** | 4.5-5.0 kPa |
| **Temperature** | Avoid fever |
| **Glucose** | Normoglycaemia |
| **Head Position** | 30° head up elevation |

## Mild Head Injury (GCS 13-15)

### Thorough Assessment Required

Document:
- Witnessed LOC or amnesia
- Mechanism of injury
- Time of injury
- Initial GCS & current GCS (trend)
- Any neurological abnormality
- Seizures
- Headache severity
- Penetrating injury/depressed skull/base of skull fracture signs
- **Elderly patients**: anticoagulants, bleeding diathesis, comorbidities
- Difficulty assessing (alcohol, epilepsy, suspicious history)
- **Neurological symptoms**: vomiting >1x, neurological deficit

---

## CT Head Criteria (NICE Guideline 176)

### CT Within 1 Hour

Perform CT within **1 hour** if ANY of:

- GCS <13 on initial assessment
- GCS <15 at 2 hours post-injury
- Suspected open or depressed skull fracture
- **Base of skull fracture signs**:
  - Haemotympanum
  - 'Panda' eyes
  - CSF leak from ear/nose
  - Battle's sign
- Post-traumatic seizure
- Focal neurological deficit
- **>1 episode of vomiting**

### CT Within 8 Hours

For patients with **amnesia or LOC** AND any of:

- Age ≥65 years
- Bleeding/clotting disorder history
- **Dangerous mechanism**:
  - Pedestrian/cyclist hit by car
  - Ejected from motor vehicle
  - Fall from >1 metre or >5 steps
- **>30 minutes retrograde amnesia**

### Anticoagulation

**Any head injury + anticoagulant treatment** = CT within 8 hours

Even if no other indication present.

---

## C-Spine CT Criteria

### CT C-Spine Within 1 Hour if:

- GCS <13 on initial assessment
- Patient intubated
- Plain X-rays technically inadequate
- Plain X-rays suspicious/abnormal
- Definitive diagnosis needed urgently (e.g., before surgery)
- Other body areas being scanned for head injury/trauma
- **Alert and stable patient** with c-spine suspicion AND:
  - Age ≥65 years
  - Dangerous mechanism (as above, plus diving/high-speed MVA/rollover)
  - Focal peripheral neurological deficit
  - Paraesthesia in limbs

---

## Neurosurgical Referral Criteria

Refer to neurosurgery if:

- Coma continuing after resuscitation
- Deterioration in consciousness or progressive neurological deficit
- Open injury - depressed fracture or penetrating injury
- **Abnormal CT**
- GCS ≤12 after resuscitation

---

## Admission Criteria

### Observation Ward or Hospital Admission if:

- Confusion/reduced consciousness at examination
- Skull fracture (simple with normal CT) or basal skull fracture signs
- Difficulty assessing (drugs, alcohol, NAI, epilepsy, suicide attempt)
- Other medical conditions (coagulation disorders)
- **Social circumstances**: no responsible adult at home

**Note**: Post-traumatic amnesia <2 minutes or transient unconsciousness with full recovery NOT necessarily indications for admission (discuss if uncertain)

---

## Discharge Criteria

**ALL must be fulfilled:**

- GCS 15
- No criteria for observation
- No indication for CT / normal CT
- Verbal and written head injury advice given
- "Red flags" explained
- GP letter completed
- **Responsible adult** available to accompany patient home

## Post-Concussion Syndrome

### Symptoms
May persist for days to weeks:
- Headaches
- Drowsiness
- Poor memory and concentration
- Tiredness
- Dizziness
- Irritability
- Clumsiness
- School/work problems

### Management
- **Warn patients and relatives**
- Reassure symptoms will improve
- Initial rest + simple analgesia
- Gradual return to normal activities
- **Avoid sporting activities** until full recovery
- Risk of further injury if return to sport too early

### Follow-up
- Symptoms persist 4-6 weeks → GP referral
- Severe symptoms at presentation → consider neurosurgical HI clinic referral
- Follow-up clinic 2-4 weeks post-injury for:
  - Ongoing significant symptoms
  - CT abnormalities (e.g., minor cerebral contusions)
  - Significant post-concussion symptoms

## Interview Key Points

### GCS Thresholds
- **GCS 8** = often intubate too late
- Motor score most sensitive in young patients
- Verbal score more sensitive in elderly

### Blood Pressure Targets
- Age-dependent BP targets to avoid hypotension
- Hypotension = secondary brain injury

### Early Intervention
- Don't wait for GCS to drop to 8
- Look for early warning signs:
  - Worsening headache
  - Increasing agitation
  - Unexplained hypertension/HR changes
  - Pupillary changes
  - New motor deficit

### CT Timing
- 1 hour vs 8 hour criteria - know the difference
- Anticoagulation = automatic 8-hour CT

## Common Interview Questions

**Q: What are the criteria for urgent CT head?**
A: GCS <13 initially, GCS <15 at 2h, basal skull fracture signs, post-traumatic seizure, focal deficit, >1 vomit, suspected depressed/open fracture.

**Q: When would you intubate a head injury patient?**
A: GCS ≤8, loss of protective reflexes, need for CT with unsafe airway, signs of herniation, respiratory failure. BUT - motor score <5 in young patient may warrant earlier intubation.

**Q: What neuroprotective measures do you implement?**
A: Avoid hypotension (age-dependent BP targets), maintain normoxia, normocapnia (4.5-5.0 kPa), avoid fever, normoglycaemia, 30° head up.

**Q: What are signs of base of skull fracture?**
A: Haemotympanum, periorbital bruising ('panda eyes'), CSF rhinorrhoea/otorrhoea, retroauricular bruising (Battle's sign).

**Q: What is a dangerous mechanism?**
A: Fall >1m or >5 steps, pedestrian/cyclist vs car, ejection from vehicle, high-speed MVA, diving injury.
