# Traumatic Brain Injury - ICU Management

## Overview

Comprehensive tiered approach to managing severe TBI (GCS ≤8) in the intensive care setting, based on ICP monitoring and cerebral perfusion optimization.

**Source**: Salford Royal NHS Foundation Trust TBI Management Guideline

---

## Initial Assessment & Stabilization

### Stage Zero: Baseline Neuroprotection

**Apply to ALL ventilated head injury patients before ICP monitoring**

| Parameter | Target | Rationale |
|-----------|--------|-----------|
| **MAP** | >80 mmHg | Maintain cerebral perfusion |
| **CPP** | Aim for ≥60 mmHg | Optimize brain tissue oxygenation |
| **PaO₂** | >10 kPa (>75 mmHg) | Avoid hypoxia |
| **PaCO₂** | 4.5-5.0 kPa (35-40 mmHg) | Normocarbia (avoid hyperventilation) |
| **Temperature** | 36-37°C | Avoid hyperthermia |
| **Glucose** | 6-10 mmol/L | Avoid hyper/hypoglycemia |
| **Sodium** | 135-145 mmol/L | Maintain normonatraemia |
| **Head Position** | 30° head up | Optimize venous drainage |
| **Neck** | Midline, avoid constriction | Ensure venous return |
| **Sedation** | Adequate depth | Reduce metabolic demand |
| **Seizure Control** | No clinical seizures | Prevent secondary injury |

### Immediate Investigations

**On Admission to ICU**:
- FBC, U&E, LFT, Coagulation
- Arterial blood gas
- CT head (if not already done)
- **ICP monitor insertion** if indicated

## ICP Monitoring Indications

### Indication for ICP Monitoring

**Consider ICP monitor if GCS ≤8 AND any of:**
- Abnormal CT (haematoma, contusion, oedema, compressed basal cisterns)
- Normal CT but ≥2 of:
  - Age >40 years
  - Unilateral or bilateral motor posturing
  - SBP <90 mmHg

### Types of ICP Monitoring
- **External ventricular drain (EVD)**: Gold standard, allows CSF drainage
- **Intraparenchymal bolt**: Alternative if EVD not feasible

### ICP Waveform Interpretation

**Normal ICP**: 5-15 mmHg (7-20 cmH₂O)
**Raised ICP**: >20 mmHg sustained

**Waveform Components**:
- **P1 (percussion wave)**: Arterial pulsation
- **P2 (tidal wave)**: Brain compliance
- **P3 (dicrotic wave)**: Aortic valve closure

**Pathological patterns**:
- **P2 > P1**: Decreased compliance (early warning sign)
- **Plateau waves (A-waves)**: Sustained ICP rise 50-100 mmHg for 5-20 min (critical)

## Tiered Management Protocol

### Tier 1: First-Line Measures

**Target**: ICP <20 mmHg, CPP 60-70 mmHg

1. **Optimize Stage Zero parameters** (see above)
2. **Sedation & Analgesia**:
   - **Propofol** infusion (4-6 mg/kg/h max)
   - **Fentanyl** or **Remifentanil**
   - Target: RASS -4 to -5
3. **CSF Drainage** (if EVD in situ):
   - Drain 2-3 ml CSF when ICP >20 mmHg
   - Re-check ICP after 5 minutes
4. **Osmotherapy** - Hypertonic Saline:
   - **2.7% NaCl**: 250 ml over 15 min
   - OR **5% NaCl**: 100 ml over 15 min
   - Repeat PRN (max 3-4 times daily)
   - Monitor sodium (target 145-155 mmol/L)
5. **Avoid hyperthermia**: Paracetamol, cooling measures

### Tier 2: Second-Line Measures

**Escalate if ICP remains >20 mmHg despite Tier 1**

Consult with neurosurgery consultant before proceeding.

1. **Neuromuscular Blockade**:
   - **Atracurium** or **Rocuronium** infusion
   - Monitor with train-of-four
   - ⚠️ Increases risk of ICU-acquired weakness
2. **Optimized Osmotherapy**:
   - **Mannitol** 0.25-1 g/kg (alternative to hypertonic saline)
   - Monitor serum osmolality (<320 mOsm/kg)
   - Maintain euvolaemia with IV fluids
3. **Therapeutic Hypothermia** (32-35°C):
   - Active cooling with cooling devices
   - Monitor for complications: arrhythmias, coagulopathy, infection
   - Slow rewarming (<0.5°C/hour)
4. **Repeat CT Head**:
   - Exclude evolving surgical lesion
   - Assess for cerebral oedema progression

### Tier 3: Third-Line Measures

**Escalate if refractory ICP elevation despite Tier 2**

⚠️ **High-risk interventions - discuss with senior neurosurgical team**

1. **Decompressive Craniectomy**:
   - **Indications**:
     - Refractory ICP >25 mmHg
     - Evidence of midline shift >5 mm
     - Young patient (<40 years)
     - Potentially salvageable brain
   - **Contraindications**:
     - Bilateral fixed dilated pupils
     - Devastating CT findings
     - Significant pre-existing disability
   - **Evidence**: DECRA trial (2011), RESCUEicp trial (2016)
2. **Barbiturate Coma**:
   - **Thiopentone** loading + infusion
   - Target: Burst suppression on EEG
   - ⚠️ Risk of hypotension, immunosuppression
   - Requires haemodynamic support
3. **Hyperventilation** (Target PaCO₂ 3.5-4.0 kPa):
   - ⚠️ TEMPORARY measure only
   - Risk of cerebral vasoconstriction → ischaemia
   - Use jugular bulb saturation (SjvO₂) monitoring if available
   - Maximum 24-48 hours

## Ventilation Strategy

### Ventilator Settings
- **Mode**: Volume control or PRVC
- **Tidal volume**: 6-8 ml/kg ideal body weight
- **PEEP**: 5-8 cmH₂O (balance between oxygenation and ICP)
- **FiO₂**: Titrate to SpO₂ >94%

### Cautions
- **High PEEP**: May increase ICP by impeding venous return
- **Low PEEP**: May cause atelectasis and hypoxia
- **Optimal PEEP**: Balance lung protection with ICP control

---

## Blood Pressure & CPP Management

### CPP Calculation
**CPP = MAP - ICP**

### CPP Targets
- **Standard target**: 60-70 mmHg
- **Avoid**: CPP <60 mmHg (cerebral ischaemia risk)
- **Caution**: CPP >70 mmHg may increase oedema in some patients

### Hypotension Management (MAP <80 mmHg)
1. **Fluid resuscitation**: 0.9% NaCl boluses
2. **Vasopressors**:
   - **Noradrenaline** (first-line)
   - **Vasopressin** (second-line)
   - Titrate to MAP target

### Hypertension Management (MAP >110 mmHg)
- Often physiological response to raised ICP
- **First**: Treat ICP elevation
- **If persistent**: Consider labetalol or other agent
- Avoid aggressive BP lowering

## Agitation Management

### Richmond Agitation-Sedation Scale (RASS)

| Score | Description | Clinical State |
|-------|-------------|----------------|
| +4 | Combative | Violent, immediate danger |
| +3 | Very agitated | Pulls/removes tubes, aggressive |
| +2 | Agitated | Frequent non-purposeful movement |
| +1 | Restless | Anxious but not aggressive |
| 0 | Alert and calm | Target for awakening |
| -1 | Drowsy | Not fully alert but sustained awakening |
| -2 | Light sedation | Brief awakening to voice |
| -3 | Moderate sedation | Movement to voice, no eye contact |
| -4 | Deep sedation | No response to voice, movement to physical |
| -5 | Unarousable | No response to physical stimulation |

### Management of Agitation (RASS +1 to +4)

**Step-wise approach**:

1. **Identify & treat reversible causes**:
   - Hypoxia, hypercapnia
   - Pain (inadequate analgesia)
   - Full bladder/bowel
   - Seizures
   - Metabolic disturbance (Na, glucose, Ca)
   - Sepsis/infection
2. **Pharmacological management**:
   - Increase sedation (propofol or midazolam)
   - Optimize analgesia (fentanyl/alfentanil)
   - **Haloperidol** 2.5-5 mg IV (if psychotic features)
   - **Dexmedetomidine** (α2-agonist, preserves respiratory drive)
3. **PSH/PAID syndrome** (see below)
4. **Physical restraints**: Last resort, requires careful monitoring

## Paroxysmal Sympathetic Hyperactivity (PSH) / PAID Syndrome

### Clinical Features

**Episodic paroxysms** of:
- Tachycardia (HR >120 bpm)
- Hypertension (SBP >140 mmHg)
- Tachypnoea (RR >30)
- Hyperthermia (T >38°C)
- Diaphoresis
- Dystonic posturing

**Triggers**: Suctioning, repositioning, tactile stimulation

### Diagnosis: PSH Assessment Measure (PSH-AM)

**Clinical Features** (score 0-2 per feature):
- Tachycardia, tachypnoea, hypertension, hyperthermia, sweating, posturing

**Diagnosis Likelihood Tool** (score 0-12):
- <8: Unlikely PSH
- 8-16: Possible PSH
- ≥17: Probable PSH

### Management

1. **Environmental**:
   - Minimize stimulation
   - Quiet, darkened room
   - Cluster care activities
2. **Pharmacological**:
   - **β-blockers**: Propranolol 20-40 mg TDS (first-line)
   - **α2-agonists**: Clonidine 50-150 mcg TDS or Dexmedetomidine infusion
   - **GABAergic agents**: Diazepam, Baclofen
   - **Opioids**: Morphine (reduces sympathetic drive)
   - **Bromocriptine**: Dopamine agonist (2.5-10 mg TDS)
3. **Avoid**:
   - Excessive stimulation
   - Sudden changes in position
   - Multiple simultaneous procedures

## Metabolic & Electrolyte Management

### Sodium Management

**Target**: 135-145 mmol/L (Stage Zero), 145-155 mmol/L (with osmotherapy)

**Hypernatraemia** (Na >145):
- Common with hypertonic saline therapy
- Acceptable if intentional (osmotherapy)
- Monitor: Aim <155 mmol/L

**Hyponatraemia** (Na <135):

**Differential diagnosis**:

| | **Cerebral Salt Wasting** | **SIADH** |
|---|---|---|
| **Volume status** | Hypovolaemic | Euvolaemic |
| **Urine output** | Polyuria (high) | Oliguria (low) |
| **Urine Na** | >40 mmol/L | >40 mmol/L |
| **Serum osmolality** | Low | Low |
| **Urine osmolality** | High | High |
| **Management** | IV 0.9% NaCl, fludrocortisone | Fluid restriction |

### Glucose Management
- **Target**: 6-10 mmol/L
- **Avoid**: Hypoglycemia (<4 mmol/L) and hyperglycemia (>10 mmol/L)
- **Insulin infusion**: VRIII if needed

### Temperature Management
- **Target**: 36-37°C
- **Fever**: Aggressive treatment (increases metabolic demand)
- **Methods**: Paracetamol, NSAIDs, cooling blankets, intravascular cooling

## Seizure Management

### Prophylaxis
- **NOT routinely recommended** for all TBI patients
- Consider for:
  - Depressed skull fracture
  - Penetrating injury
  - Early post-traumatic seizure (<7 days)
  - Intracerebral haematoma

### Treatment of Seizures

**Clinical seizures**:
1. **Lorazepam** 4 mg IV (or **Midazolam** 10 mg IM)
2. **Levetiracetam** 1.5 g IV loading (then 500 mg BD)
3. If refractory → **Phenytoin** 15-20 mg/kg IV loading

**Status epilepticus**: See [[Status Epilepticus Management]]

### Subclinical Seizures
- Consider **EEG monitoring** in comatose patients
- Especially if unexplained deterioration

---

## Nutrition & GI Management

### Nutrition
- **Early enteral nutrition**: Within 24-48 hours
- **Route**: Nasogastric or post-pyloric tube
- **Target**: 25-30 kcal/kg/day, 1.2-2 g protein/kg/day
- **Monitoring**: Gastric residual volumes, tolerance

### Gastric Protection
- **Proton pump inhibitor**: Omeprazole 40 mg OD or Lansoprazole 30 mg OD
- Stress ulcer prophylaxis

### Bowel Management
- **Laxatives**: Lactulose, senna (prevent constipation/straining)
- Monitor for bowel dysfunction post-injury

---

## DVT Prophylaxis

### Mechanical Prophylaxis
- **Intermittent pneumatic compression** (IPC) devices: Start immediately
- **TED stockings**: If appropriate

### Pharmacological Prophylaxis
- **Timing**: Start 24-48 hours post-injury if:
  - No active bleeding
  - Stable CT head
  - No planned surgery
- **Agent**: LMWH (enoxaparin 40 mg SC OD or dalteparin 5000 units SC OD)
- **Caution**: Balance bleeding vs thrombosis risk

---

## Prognostication

### Timing
- **Avoid early prognostication** (<72 hours)
- **Optimal timing**: 7-10 days post-injury
- Consider at least 5-7 days of stable ICU care before discussing prognosis

### Poor Prognostic Factors

**Clinical**:
- Older age (>60 years)
- Low GCS (especially motor score 1-2)
- Bilateral pupillary unreactivity
- Prolonged hypotension/hypoxia

**Radiological**:
- Diffuse axonal injury
- Brainstem injury
- Midline shift >5 mm
- Obliteration of basal cisterns
- Marshall CT classification grade V-VI

**Physiological**:
- Refractory ICP elevation
- CPP <50 mmHg sustained
- Global cerebral oedema

### Prognostic Tools
- **CRASH-CT**: Online calculator (British Journal of Neurosurgery 2011)
- **IMPACT**: Prognostic model (PLoS Med 2008)

### Important Considerations
- **Self-fulfilling prophecy**: Early withdrawal may bias outcomes
- **Confounders**: Sedation, metabolic factors
- **Delayed improvement**: Some patients improve over weeks-months
- **Discuss with senior team** before prognostic discussions with family

## Weaning Sedation & Extubation

### Criteria for Sedation Hold
- ICP <20 mmHg for >24 hours
- No surgical intervention planned
- Haemodynamically stable
- No active seizures

### Sedation Weaning
- **Gradual reduction**: 10-20% per day
- **Monitor ICP closely**: During weaning
- **Re-escalate if**: ICP rises or patient agitated

### Extubation Criteria
- GCS >8 (motor score ≥5)
- Adequate cough and gag
- Can protect airway
- Stable ICP if monitor still in situ
- Haemodynamically stable
- No significant respiratory compromise

### Tracheostomy
- Consider if:
  - Prolonged ventilation expected (>7-10 days)
  - Failed extubation
  - Poor airway protection
  - Severe facial/skull base fractures

---

## Withdrawal of ICP Monitor

### Timing
- ICP <20 mmHg for >48 hours
- Patient clinically improving
- Off sedation or sedation weaning progressing
- Infection risk (prolonged monitoring >5-7 days)

### Monitoring after removal
- Close neurological observations
- Low threshold for repeat CT if deterioration

## Key Evidence Base

### Landmark Trials & Guidelines

1. **Brain Trauma Foundation Guidelines (4th Edition, 2016)**
   - ICP monitoring and threshold recommendations
   - CPP optimization strategies
   - Level III evidence for most interventions

2. **DECRA Trial (2011)**
   - New England Journal of Medicine
   - Decompressive craniectomy for refractory ICP
   - **Result**: Improved ICP control but worse functional outcomes at 6 months
   - **Criticism**: Early decompression, not truly "refractory"

3. **RESCUEicp Trial (2016)**
   - NEJM 2016
   - Decompressive craniectomy for refractory intracranial hypertension
   - **Result**: Reduced mortality but increased severe disability
   - **Conclusion**: Offers survival but with disability trade-off

4. **SAFE Study (2004)**
   - Saline vs albumin in fluid resuscitation
   - **TBI subgroup**: Trend toward harm with albumin

5. **Eurotherm3235 Trial (2015)**
   - Therapeutic hypothermia for ICP
   - **Result**: No benefit, possible harm

6. **BEST TRIP Trial (2012)**
   - ICP monitoring vs clinical exam + imaging in resource-limited settings
   - **Result**: Similar outcomes, but ICP monitoring allows protocolized care

## Interview Key Points

### Management Principles

1. **Stage Zero neuroprotection** for ALL ventilated TBI patients
2. **Tiered escalation** based on ICP response
3. **Balance CPP and ICP**: CPP 60-70 mmHg, ICP <20 mmHg
4. **Avoid secondary insults**: Hypotension, hypoxia, hypercarbia, fever
5. **Early intervention**: Don't wait for catastrophic ICP rise
6. **Multidisciplinary approach**: Neurosurgery, ICU, therapy teams

### Common Interview Questions

**Q: What is your approach to a ventilated patient with severe TBI?**
A: Start with Stage Zero neuroprotection (optimize MAP, CPP, oxygenation, normocarbia, normothermia, normoglycemia, head position). Insert ICP monitor if GCS ≤8 with abnormal CT or risk factors. Use tiered approach to manage ICP: Tier 1 (sedation, CSF drainage, osmotherapy), Tier 2 (paralysis, therapeutic hypothermia), Tier 3 (decompressive craniectomy, barbiturate coma). Target ICP <20 mmHg, CPP 60-70 mmHg.

**Q: What is the role of decompressive craniectomy in TBI?**
A: Decompressive craniectomy is a Tier 3 intervention for refractory intracranial hypertension despite maximal medical therapy. DECRA (2011) showed improved ICP control but worse functional outcomes. RESCUEicp (2016) showed reduced mortality but increased severe disability. It's a life-saving procedure but with significant morbidity. Discuss with patient/family regarding quality of life vs survival.

**Q: How do you differentiate cerebral salt wasting from SIADH?**
A: Both cause hyponatraemia. CSW: hypovolaemic with high urine output → treat with IV saline. SIADH: euvolaemic with low urine output → treat with fluid restriction. Measurement of urine sodium, serum/urine osmolality, and volume status helps differentiate.

**Q: What are the indications for ICP monitoring?**
A: GCS ≤8 with abnormal CT (haematoma, contusion, oedema, compressed cisterns) OR normal CT with ≥2 of: age >40, motor posturing, SBP <90 mmHg.

**Q: Describe your osmotherapy strategy.**
A: First-line: Hypertonic saline (2.7% 250ml or 5% 100ml) PRN for ICP >20. Target sodium 145-155. Alternative: Mannitol 0.25-1 g/kg (monitor osmolality <320). Hypertonic saline preferred as doesn't cause diuresis and can be repeated more frequently.

**Q: What is paroxysmal sympathetic hyperactivity (PSH)?**
A: Previously called "autonomic storming" - episodic sympathetic surges with tachycardia, hypertension, hyperthermia, sweating, posturing. Triggered by stimulation. Diagnosed with PSH-AM score. Treat with β-blockers (propranolol), α2-agonists (clonidine/dexmedetomidine), minimize stimulation, GABAergic agents.

**Q: When would you consider barbiturate coma?**
A: Tier 3, last-resort measure for refractory ICP elevation despite Tier 1 and 2 interventions, often after considering decompressive craniectomy. Thiopentone loading with EEG burst suppression target. High risk of hypotension and immunosuppression. Not routinely recommended due to significant adverse effects.

**Q: What is your approach to ventilation in TBI?**
A: Target normocarbia (PaCO₂ 4.5-5.0 kPa), avoid hyperventilation except as temporary Tier 3 measure. Use lung-protective strategies (TV 6-8 ml/kg). Balance PEEP (5-8 cmH₂O) between oxygenation and venous return. Avoid high PEEP that may increase ICP.

**Q: How do you manage agitation in TBI?**
A: Use RASS scale. First identify reversible causes (pain, hypoxia, full bladder, metabolic, seizures). Optimize sedation/analgesia. Consider PSH if sympathetic features. Pharmacology: propofol, fentanyl, dexmedetomidine, haloperidol. Avoid excessive physical restraint.
