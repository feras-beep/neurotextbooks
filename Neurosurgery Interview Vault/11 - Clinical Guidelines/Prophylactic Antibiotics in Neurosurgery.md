# Prophylactic Antibiotics in Neurosurgery

## Overview

Comprehensive antibiotic prophylaxis protocol for neurosurgical procedures to prevent postoperative infections including meningitis, wound infections, and implant-related infections.

**Source**: Cambridge University Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust (Version 6, August 2019)

---

## Key Evidence

### Efficacy of Antibiotic Prophylaxis

**Meta-analysis findings:**
- Prophylactic antibiotics reduce postoperative **meningitis by ~50%**
- Significant reduction (~50%) in infection rates for craniotomy and shunt surgery
- **Baseline infection rate**: 2-6%
- **Number Needed to Treat (NNT)**: 14-16 patients to prevent one infection

### Common Pathogens

| Organism | Prevalence |
|----------|------------|
| **Staphylococcus aureus** | 52% |
| **Coagulase-negative staphylococci** | 15% |
| **Streptococci** | 9% |
| **Gram-negative bacilli** | 16% |
| **Others** | 8% |

**Rationale for flucloxacillin + gentamicin:** Most organisms susceptible, narrow spectrum (antimicrobial stewardship)

---

## Critical Timing

### NICE Recommendations

**Optimal timing:** At **beginning of anaesthesia** (induction)

**Infection risk increases significantly when:**
- Prophylaxis given **after surgical incision**
- Prophylaxis given **>2 hours before incision**

### Repeat Dosing During Long Operations

Additional doses recommended when **operation duration > antibiotic half-life**

⚠️ **Important update:** Repeat gentamicin doses NOT recommended for long operations

## Clean Implant & Non-Implant Procedures

### Definition
Surgery through **healthy skin** (craniofacial and spinal)

### Non-Implant Procedures
- Percutaneous radiofrequency ablation (trigeminal neuralgia)
- Non-implant spinal surgery

### Implant Procedures
- CSF shunts
- Bone flaps (acrylic/titanium/bone)
- External CSF drains
- Deep brain stimulators
- Spinal cord stimulators
- Spinal implant surgery

---

### First Choice: Flucloxacillin + Gentamicin

**Adults:**
- **Flucloxacillin 2g IV** (single dose at induction)
- **PLUS Gentamicin 3mg/kg IV** (single dose at induction)

**Paediatrics (1 month - 18 years):**
- **Flucloxacillin 50mg/kg IV** (max 2g)
- **PLUS Gentamicin IV** at induction:
  - 1 month - 12 years: 2.5mg/kg (max 120mg)
  - 12-18 years: 2mg/kg (max 120mg)

---

### Second Choice: Vancomycin + Gentamicin

**Indications:**
- **Beta-lactam allergy** (minor or major)
- **History of MRSA carriage**
- **Ward outbreak of MRSA**

**Adults:**
- **Vancomycin 15mg/kg IV** (max 2g)
- **PLUS Gentamicin 3mg/kg IV**

**Paediatrics:**

| Age Group | Vancomycin Dose |
|-----------|-----------------|
| 1 month - 6 months | 10mg/kg |
| 6 months - 1 year | 15mg/kg |
| 1 - 12 years | 17.5mg/kg |
| 12-16 years | 15mg/kg |
| 17-18 years | As adults |

**PLUS Gentamicin:**
- 1 month - 12 years: 2.5mg/kg (max 120mg)
- 12-18 years: 2mg/kg (max 120mg)

## Clean Contaminated Procedures

### Definition
- **Transmucosal procedures** (endoscopic, endonasal)
- **Acoustic neuroma surgery**
- **Clean procedures in neuro-oncology patients** (high risk for *Propionibacterium* species)

**Mucous membrane prep:** Irrigation with aqueous chlorhexidine

---

### First Choice: Co-amoxiclav + Gentamicin

**Adults:**
- **Co-amoxiclav 1.2g IV** at induction
- **PLUS Gentamicin 3mg/kg IV** at induction

**Paediatrics:**
- **1 month - 12 years:**
  - Co-amoxiclav 30mg/kg IV
  - PLUS Gentamicin 2.5mg/kg IV (max 120mg)

- **12-18 years:**
  - Co-amoxiclav 30mg/kg IV (max 1.2g)
  - PLUS Gentamicin 2mg/kg IV (max 120mg)

---

### Second Choice: Vancomycin + Gentamicin + Metronidazole

**Indications:**
- Beta-lactam allergy
- MRSA carriage/outbreak

**Adults:**
- **Vancomycin 15mg/kg IV** (max 2g)
- **PLUS Gentamicin 3mg/kg IV**
- **PLUS Metronidazole 500mg IV**

**Paediatrics:**
- Vancomycin (age-based dosing as above)
- PLUS Gentamicin (age-based as above)
- PLUS Metronidazole 7.5mg/kg IV (max 500mg)

## Special Situations

### 1. CSF Shunts - Special Protocols

#### Shunt Irrigation
- Irrigate shunts in **gentamicin before insertion**
- Efficacy in infection prevention: **unproven**

#### Before VP Shunt (If EVD Present)
**Instill into ventricles:**
- **Vancomycin 20mg**
- **PLUS Gentamicin 5mg**

#### EVD/Lumbar Drain Management
- **Never manipulate** drains except for clinical reasons
- **Full aseptic precautions** always
- **Remove as soon as possible** to minimize meningitis risk

---

### 2. Skull Fracture & CSF Fistula

#### Management Principles
- **Urgent assessment** required
- Surgical repair may be indicated
- **❌ Antibiotics NOT recommended** (except for surgical repair)

#### Vaccination Protocol

**Adults:**
- **PPV23 (Pneumovax II)** 0.5ml IM (single dose)

**Children (>2 years):**
- If completed PCV13 (Prevenar-13) schedule:
  - Give **PPV23** at least **2 months** after last PCV13 dose
- If unvaccinated/partially vaccinated:
  - Refer to Green Book Chapter 25

**Rationale:** Protect against *Streptococcus pneumoniae* meningitis

---

### 3. Penetrating Craniocerebral Injury

**Duration:** 5 days total (3 days IV, 2 days PO)

#### First Choice: Ceftriaxone + Metronidazole

**Adults:**
- **Ceftriaxone 2g IV** once daily
- **PLUS Metronidazole 500mg IV** TDS (8-hourly)
- **After 3 days:** Step down to **co-amoxiclav 625mg PO** TDS for 2 days

**Paediatrics (1 month - 12 years):**
- **Ceftriaxone 50mg/kg IV** once daily (max 2g)
- **PLUS Metronidazole 7.5mg/kg IV** TDS (max 500mg)
  - ⚠️ If <2 months: metronidazole 12-hourly (not 8-hourly)

**12-18 years:**
- As adults

#### Second Choice: Vancomycin + Ciprofloxacin + Metronidazole

**For major beta-lactam allergy**

**Adults:**
- **Vancomycin 15mg/kg IV** (max 2g) 12-hourly
- **PLUS Ciprofloxacin 400mg IV** 12-hourly
- **PLUS Metronidazole 500mg IV** TDS

**Paediatrics:**
- Vancomycin (age-based, 6-hourly dosing)
- PLUS Ciprofloxacin 10mg/kg IV (max 400mg) TDS
- PLUS Metronidazole 7.5mg/kg IV (max 500mg) TDS

#### Additional Measures
- **Tetanus prophylaxis:** Human anti-tetanus immunoglobulin (250-500 IU IM)
- Update tetanus vaccination status
- Surgical debridement of devitalized tissue essential

#### Historical Context
- **Pre-antibiotic era:** Infection rate high, mortality ~50%
- **With broad-spectrum antibiotics:** Infection rate 1-5%
- **Risk factors:** CSF leaks, wound sinuses, ventricular involvement

## Important Clinical Notes

### Note 1: Aseptic Technique
- **Most important factor** in preventing infection
- Hair removal: Electric clippers with single-use head (day of surgery)
- Skin prep: Alcohol-containing solution, avoid pooling (flammable!)
- Wait ≥30 seconds before proceeding

### Note 2: Prolonged/High-Risk Operations
**Antibiotics may be continued up to 24 hours post-op in:**
- Prolonged and difficult operations
- High-risk procedures (elderly, malignancy)
- Complex spinal surgery

### Note 3: CSF Fistula/Leaks
⚠️ **NO benefit** for antibiotic prophylaxis in CSF fistula
- Only give antibiotics if surgical repair

### Note 4: Post-Operative Dosing
**Literature review:** No significant benefit for post-operative doses in:
- General surgery
- Orthopaedic implant surgery
- By implication: Neurosurgery

**Exception:** May continue for max 24 hours in prolonged/difficult cases

### Note 5: Optimized Dosing (Updated Guideline)
- **Flucloxacillin increased to 2g** (from 1g)
- **Gentamicin increased to 3mg/kg** (from lower dose)
- **No repeat gentamicin** for long operations

---

## Repeat Dosing During Long Operations

### When to Redose
When operation duration **> antibiotic half-life**

Redose at intervals of **1-2 times the half-life**

### Adult Intra-Operative Repeat Dosing

| Antibiotic | Repeat Dose | Interval |
|------------|-------------|----------|
| **Flucloxacillin** | 1g IV | 4-hourly |
| **Co-amoxiclav** | 600mg IV | 4-hourly |
| **Vancomycin** | 500mg IV | 8-hourly |
| **Metronidazole** | 500mg IV | 6-8 hourly |
| **Ciprofloxacin** | 400mg IV | 12-hourly |
| **Gentamicin** | ❌ DO NOT repeat | N/A |

---

## Post-Operative Continuation (Up to 24 Hours)

### For Prolonged/Difficult Operations Only

**Adults:**

| Antibiotic | Dose | Frequency |
|------------|------|-----------|
| **Flucloxacillin** | 1g IV | 6-hourly |
| **Co-amoxiclav** | 1.2g IV | 8-hourly |
| **Vancomycin** | 15mg/kg IV (max 2g) | 12-hourly |
| **Metronidazole** | 500mg IV | 8-hourly |
| **Ciprofloxacin** | 400mg IV | 12-hourly |

**Maximum duration:** 24 hours post-op

## Interview Key Points

### Why Prophylactic Antibiotics?
- Reduces meningitis by ~50%
- Reduces overall infection by ~50%
- NNT = 14-16 patients
- Most effective adjunct to aseptic technique

### Flucloxacillin + Gentamicin Rationale
- Covers **Staph aureus** (52% of infections)
- Covers coagulase-negative staph (15%)
- **Narrow spectrum** (antimicrobial stewardship)
- Most organisms susceptible
- Low cost

### Why NOT Continue Post-Op?
- No proven benefit in literature
- Increases antibiotic resistance
- Baseline infection rates already low (2-6%)
- Most important = aseptic technique

### Special Populations

**MRSA Carriage:**
- Switch to **vancomycin + gentamicin**

**Beta-Lactam Allergy:**
- **Minor or major:** Use vancomycin-based regimen

**Neuro-Oncology:**
- Use **co-amoxiclav** (covers *Propionibacterium*)

**Penetrating Injury:**
- **5-day course** (not single dose)
- Broader spectrum (ceftriaxone + metronidazole)

## Common Interview Questions

**Q: What is your antibiotic prophylaxis for routine craniotomy?**

A:
- **Flucloxacillin 2g IV** + **Gentamicin 3mg/kg IV** at induction
- **Single dose** (no post-op continuation)
- Given at **beginning of anaesthesia** (critical timing)
- If beta-lactam allergy or MRSA: Vancomycin 15mg/kg + Gentamicin 3mg/kg

**Q: Why do we give antibiotics at induction, not earlier?**

A:
- **Optimal timing** = beginning of anaesthesia
- Ensures adequate tissue levels during surgery
- Too early (>2 hours before): Levels drop below MIC
- Too late (after incision): Bacteria already contaminated wound
- Evidence from meta-analysis of 54,552 patients (De Jonge 2017)

**Q: Do you continue antibiotics post-operatively after craniotomy?**

A:
- **Generally NO** - single dose sufficient
- Literature shows no benefit for post-op doses
- **Exception:** May continue up to 24 hours in:
  - Prolonged/difficult operations
  - High-risk patients (elderly, malignancy)
  - Complex spinal instrumentation

**Q: What is your prophylaxis for VP shunt insertion?**

A:
- Same as clean implant surgery: **Flucloxacillin 2g + Gentamicin 3mg/kg**
- **Additional measures:**
  - Irrigate shunt in gentamicin before insertion (efficacy unproven)
  - If EVD present before shunt: Instill vancomycin 20mg + gentamicin 5mg into ventricles
- **Single dose** at induction

**Q: How do you manage a patient with penetrating head injury?**

A:
- **5-day antibiotic course** (not prophylaxis - this is treatment)
- **Ceftriaxone 2g IV OD** + **Metronidazole 500mg IV TDS**
- Step down after 3 days IV to co-amoxiclav 625mg PO TDS for 2 days
- **Additional measures:**
  - Tetanus immunoglobulin (250-500 IU IM)
  - Update tetanus vaccination
  - Surgical debridement essential
- Pre-antibiotic era: 50% mortality; with antibiotics: 1-5% infection rate

**Q: What antibiotics for transsphenoidal surgery?**

A:
- **Clean contaminated** procedure (transmucosal)
- **Co-amoxiclav 1.2g IV** + **Gentamicin 3mg/kg IV** at induction
- Irrigate mucous membranes with aqueous chlorhexidine
- If beta-lactam allergy: Vancomycin + Gentamicin + Metronidazole

**Q: A patient with MRSA carriage needs craniotomy - what prophylaxis?**

A:
- **Vancomycin 15mg/kg IV** (max 2g)
- **PLUS Gentamicin 3mg/kg IV**
- Single dose at induction
- Same regimen for ward MRSA outbreak

**Q: Do you give antibiotics for CSF rhinorrhoea?**

A:
- **NO** - antibiotics have no proven benefit for CSF fistula/leak
- **Management:**
  - Urgent assessment for surgical repair
  - If surgery required: Standard surgical prophylaxis
  - **Pneumococcal vaccination** (PPV23) essential
- Vaccinate to prevent *S. pneumoniae* meningitis

**Q: When do you give repeat intra-operative doses?**

A:
- When **operation duration > antibiotic half-life**
- Give at intervals of 1-2 times half-life
- **Examples:**
  - Flucloxacillin: Repeat 1g every 4 hours
  - Co-amoxiclav: Repeat 600mg every 4 hours
  - Vancomycin: Repeat 500mg every 8 hours
- **⚠️ DO NOT repeat gentamicin**

**Q: What are the most common organisms in neurosurgical infections?**

A:
- **Staph aureus** (52%) - most common
- Coagulase-negative staphylococci (15%)
- Gram-negative bacilli (16%)
- Streptococci (9%)
- Others (8%)
- Rationale for flucloxacillin + gentamicin: Covers most pathogens

**Q: What is the NNT for antibiotic prophylaxis in craniotomy?**

A:
- **NNT = 14-16 patients** to prevent one infection
- Baseline infection rate: 2-6%
- Prophylaxis reduces infection by ~50%
- Meta-analysis (Barker 2007): Reduces meningitis by approximately half

**Q: Why co-amoxiclav for neuro-oncology patients?**

A:
- Neuro-oncology patients at high risk for **Propionibacterium** infections
- Co-amoxiclav provides **broader spectrum** including anaerobes
- Standard flucloxacillin regimen inadequate coverage

**Q: What is antimicrobial stewardship rationale for this protocol?**

A:
- **Narrow spectrum** antibiotics preferred (flucloxacillin + gentamicin)
- **Single dose** (not prolonged courses)
- No post-operative continuation (unless high-risk)
- Target most common organisms
- Minimize resistance development
- Switch to vancomycin only when indicated (MRSA/allergy)

## Practical Prescribing Summary

### Standard Craniotomy (No Allergy, No MRSA)
```
At induction:
1. Flucloxacillin 2g IV (slow bolus)
2. Gentamicin 3mg/kg IV (over 5 minutes)
```

### VP Shunt Insertion
```
At induction:
1. Flucloxacillin 2g IV
2. Gentamicin 3mg/kg IV

Intra-operative (if EVD present):
3. Vancomycin 20mg intraventricular
4. Gentamicin 5mg intraventricular
```

### Transsphenoidal Surgery
```
At induction:
1. Co-amoxiclav 1.2g IV
2. Gentamicin 3mg/kg IV

Pre-operative:
3. Irrigate nasal mucosa with aqueous chlorhexidine
```

### Penetrating Head Injury
```
Day 1-3 (IV):
1. Ceftriaxone 2g IV OD
2. Metronidazole 500mg IV TDS

Day 4-5 (PO):
3. Co-amoxiclav 625mg PO TDS

Plus:
4. Tetanus immunoglobulin 250-500 IU IM (once)
5. Update tetanus vaccination
```

### CSF Rhinorrhoea (Conservative Management)
```
NO antibiotics

Vaccination:
1. PPV23 (Pneumovax II) 0.5ml IM (single dose)
```

## Red Flags & Contraindications

### DO NOT Give Prophylaxis
❌ CSF fistula/leak (no evidence of benefit)
❌ Chronic subdural hematoma drainage (not mentioned in protocol)

### Relative Contraindications
⚠️ **Gentamicin:**
- Renal impairment (dose adjust)
- Concurrent nephrotoxic drugs
- Previous gentamicin toxicity

⚠️ **Vancomycin:**
- "Red man syndrome" (infuse slowly)
- Renal impairment (monitor levels)

⚠️ **Beta-lactams:**
- True anaphylaxis (use vancomycin alternative)
- Minor allergy may still tolerate
