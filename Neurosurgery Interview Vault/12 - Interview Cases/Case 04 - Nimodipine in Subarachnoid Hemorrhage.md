# Case 04 - Nimodipine in Subarachnoid Hemorrhage

## Case Presentation

**Patient:** 38-year-old man

**Presentation:**
- Admitted acutely following an **aneurysmal subarachnoid haemorrhage**
- Started on regular nimodipine by the on-call neurosurgical SHO

**Medication shown:** Nimotop® 30mg Tablets (Nimodipine) with molecular structure

---

## Questions & Model Answers

### 1. What dosage, frequency and duration of treatment should the patient receive?

## Nimodipine Dosing Protocol

**Standard Regimen:**
- **60mg of oral nimodipine** every four hours

**Duration:**
- **21 days from ictus**

### Dose Modifications

**If blood pressure is low:**
- Reduce to **30mg every 2 hours** if SBP <90 mmHg
- Continue monitoring BP closely
- Balance risk of vasospasm vs hypotension

**Route:**
- **Oral** preferred
- Can be given via NG tube if patient unable to swallow
- **IV formulation** available but has significant complications (avoid if possible)

### Practical Considerations

**Timing:**
- Start as soon as diagnosis confirmed
- Continue for full 21 days regardless of aneurysm treatment
- Do NOT stop if patient develops vasospasm
- Do NOT stop if patient deteriorates

**Monitoring:**
- Regular BP measurements (at least QDS)
- Adjust dose based on BP response
- Watch for side effects

---

### 2. What is the evidence that this regimen will benefit the patient?

## Evidence Base for Nimodipine

### British Aneurysm Nimodipine Trial

**Key Trial:** Class I evidence for nimodipine prophylaxis in the prevention of **cerebral vasospasm** for patients who have experienced aneurysmal subarachnoid haemorrhage.

**Reference:** Pickard JD et al., BMJ 1989; **298**: 636-642

### Trial Design

The **British aneurysm nimodipine trial** was a:
- **Prospective**
- **Double-blinded**
- **Multi-centre**
- **Randomised controlled trial** of nimodipine versus placebo

**Trial Parameters:**

| Parameter | Further Details |
|-----------|-----------------|
| **Subjects** | 554 (278 nimodipine, 276 placebo) |
| **Inclusion Criteria** | • <96 hours since ictus<br>• Proven SAH (CT or LP positive)<br>• Age > 18 years |
| **Exclusion Criteria** | • ≥96 hours since ictus<br>• Pregnancy<br>• Major co-morbidities (hepatic, renal, lung, cardiac)<br>• Coma due to SAH within 7 days<br>• Lack of consent |
| **Primary Endpoint** | Rate of cerebral infarction, re-bleed or poor outcome |
| **Secondary Endpoints** | Glasgow Outcome Score ≥ 3 months after ictus |
| **Dosing** | 60mg PO QID for 21 days |

### Results

**Primary Outcome:**
- **Reduced incidence of vasospasm**:
  - Placebo: 33%
  - Nimodipine: 22%
  - **p = 0.014** (statistically significant)

**Secondary Outcome:**
- **Reduced incidence of poor outcome:**
  - Placebo: 33%
  - Nimodipine: 20%
  - **p < 0.001** (highly significant)

### Clinical Impact

This trial established nimodipine as **standard of care** for SAH prophylaxis worldwide.

**Number Needed to Treat (NNT):**
- Approximately **9** patients need to be treated to prevent one poor outcome

### 3. What is nimodipine's mechanism of action in vasospasm prevention?

## Pharmacology of Nimodipine

### Classification
**Nimodipine is a calcium channel blocker.**

### Mechanism in Vasospasm Prevention

**Primary Effect:**
It impairs the **influx of calcium ions** into cardiovascular smooth muscle cells.

**Vascular Selectivity:**
The effects of nimodipine are **more pronounced in cerebrovascular tissue** than other vessels.

**Proposed Mechanism:**
- This selectivity is thought to be due to the fact that **the molecule is lipophilic**
- Allows it to **cross the blood-brain barrier relatively freely**
- Achieves higher concentrations in cerebral vessels

### Additional Mechanisms (Theoretical)

Beyond simple calcium channel blockade:

1. **Neuroprotection:**
   - May reduce calcium-mediated neuronal injury
   - Reduces excitotoxicity

2. **Anti-inflammatory effects:**
   - May reduce inflammatory response to blood in subarachnoid space

3. **Improved microcirculation:**
   - Effects on small vessels and capillaries
   - May improve cerebral blood flow beyond just preventing large vessel spasm

4. **Metabolic effects:**
   - May protect against ischemic injury
   - Reduces free radical formation

### Why Nimodipine Over Other Calcium Channel Blockers?

**Cerebral selectivity:**
- **Lipophilic** - crosses BBB easily
- Preferentially affects **cerebral arteries** over systemic circulation
- Less profound systemic hypotension compared to other CCBs

**Evidence base:**
- Extensive trial data specifically for SAH
- Other CCBs (amlodipine, diltiazem, verapamil) not proven effective for SAH

### 4. Are there any contra-indications to nimodipine therapy?

## Contraindications to Nimodipine

### Absolute Contraindications

1. **Use within 1 month of myocardial infarction**
   - Risk of further cardiac ischemia
   - Impaired cardiac output

2. **Unstable angina**
   - May worsen cardiac ischemia
   - Reduce coronary perfusion pressure

3. **Acute porphyria**
   - Can precipitate acute porphyric crisis
   - Contraindicated in all porphyrias

### Relative Contraindications / Cautions

**Pregnancy:**
- Use advised **if potential benefits outweigh risks**
- Limited data on safety in pregnancy
- Category C drug

**Breastfeeding:**
- **No available evidence** of effects when breast feeding
- Likely secreted in breast milk
- Use with caution

**Hepatic Impairment:**
- Nimodipine extensively metabolized by liver
- May need dose reduction
- Monitor closely

**Hypotension:**
- Reduce dose if SBP <90 mmHg
- Risk of cerebral hypoperfusion
- Balance against vasospasm risk

**Concurrent Antihypertensives:**
- Additive hypotensive effects
- May need to adjust doses
- Monitor BP closely

### Drug Interactions

**Concurrent use cautions:**

| Drug Class | Effect | Management |
|------------|--------|------------|
| **Phenobarbital, phenytoin, carbamazepine** | Induce CYP3A4<br>Reduce nimodipine levels | Should not be used concurrently |
| **Other antihypertensives** | Additive hypotension | Monitor BP, adjust doses |
| **CYP3A4 inhibitors** (macrolides, azoles) | Increase nimodipine levels | Monitor for side effects |
| **Grapefruit juice** | Inhibits CYP3A4<br>Increases levels | Avoid concurrent use |

**Interestingly:**
- It is worthy of note that dexamethasone may affect phenytoin or carbamazepine levels
- These should be monitored during treatment

## Additional Clinical Pearls

### Nimodipine Administration

**Route Preferences:**
1. **Oral** - preferred route
2. **NG tube** - if unable to swallow (flush well before and after)
3. **IV** - avoid if possible due to complications

**IV Nimodipine Risks:**
- Risk of severe hypotension
- Must be given via **central line**
- **Never give as IV bolus** (can be fatal)
- Requires continuous BP monitoring
- Only use if absolutely no enteral access

### Monitoring During Therapy

**Essential Monitoring:**
- Blood pressure (at least QDS)
- Neurological status (hourly initially)
- Fluid balance
- Sodium levels (risk of hyponatraemia in SAH)
- Signs of vasospasm

**Adjust dose if:**
- SBP consistently <90 mmHg → reduce to 30mg Q2H
- Signs of vasospasm develop → continue full dose, consider hypertensive therapy
- Severe side effects develop → discuss with senior

### Side Effects

**Common:**
- Hypotension (10-20%)
- Headache
- Flushing
- Peripheral edema
- Nausea

**Less Common:**
- Bradycardia
- Dizziness
- Gastrointestinal upset
- Rash

**Serious:**
- Severe hypotension
- Cardiac ischemia (in susceptible patients)
- Hepatotoxicity (rare)

## Vasospasm in SAH - Comprehensive Overview

### Incidence and Timing

**Vasospasm affects:**
- **46% of SAH patients** (angiographic vasospasm)
- **30%** develop symptomatic/delayed cerebral ischemia (DCI)

**Timing:**
- **Onset**: Day 3-5 post-ictus
- **Peak**: Days 7-10
- **Resolution**: Days 14-21

### Risk Factors for Vasospasm

| Factor | Association |
|--------|-------------|
| **Large volume SAH** | Strong predictor (Fisher grade) |
| **Younger age** | Increased risk |
| **Smoking** | Increased risk |
| **Hypertension** | Increased risk |
| **Female sex** | Possible increased risk |
| **Intraventricular blood** | Increased risk |

### Fisher Grading Scale

| Grade | CT Appearance | Risk of Vasospasm |
|-------|---------------|-------------------|
| **1** | No blood detected | Low (~20%) |
| **2** | Diffuse thin layer (<1mm) | Low (~20%) |
| **3** | Localized clot >1mm thick | **High (~70-80%)** |
| **4** | Intraventricular or intraparenchymal blood | Moderate (~40%) |

**Modified Fisher Scale:** Now more commonly used (0-4), based on presence/absence of SAH and IVH.

### Clinical Features of Vasospasm

**Classic Triad:**
1. **Decreasing or fluctuating level of consciousness**
2. **Focal neurological signs** (stroke-like symptoms)
3. **Increasing headache**

**Subtle Signs:**
- Confusion
- Decreased GCS by 2 points
- New aphasia
- Hemiparesis
- Neglect

### Management of Vasospasm

**Prevention:**
1. **Nimodipine** 60mg QID × 21 days (evidence-based)
2. **Maintain euvolaemia** (NOT hypervolaemia)
3. **Avoid hyponatraemia**
4. **Maintain adequate CPP**

**Treatment of Symptomatic Vasospasm:**

**Triple-H Therapy (Historical - now modified):**
- **Hypertension** - induced hypertension (main component)
- ~~Hypervolaemia~~ - **now aim for EUVOLAEMIA**
- ~~Haemodilution~~ - no longer recommended

**Modern Approach:**
1. **Optimize BP** - induce hypertension
   - Target SBP 160-200 mmHg (or 20-40 mmHg above baseline)
   - Use vasopressors (norepinephrine, phenylephrine)

2. **Endovascular therapy:**
   - **Intra-arterial vasodilators** (verapamil, nicardipine, milrinone)
   - **Angioplasty** for large vessel spasm

3. **Maintain euvolaemia:**
   - **NOT hypervolaemia** (risk of pulmonary edema, cerebral edema)
   - Use CVP/fluid responsiveness to guide

4. **Supportive measures:**
   - Continue nimodipine
   - Avoid hypotension
   - Maintain normoglycaemia
   - Correct electrolytes

**Monitoring for Vasospasm:**
- **CT perfusion** - reduced CBF, increased MTT
- **Transcranial Doppler** (TCD) - elevated velocities (>120 cm/s MCA)
- **CT angiography** - vessel narrowing
- **Digital subtraction angiography** (DSA) - gold standard

## Common Interview Questions

**Q: Why is nimodipine given for 21 days?**

A: Vasospasm risk period:
- Onset: Day 3-5
- Peak: Days 7-10
- Resolution: Days 14-21
- 21-day course covers entire risk period
- Evidence from trials used 21-day protocol

**Q: What is the difference between angiographic vasospasm and delayed cerebral ischemia?**

A:
- **Angiographic vasospasm**: Radiological narrowing of vessels (46% of patients)
- **Delayed cerebral ischemia (DCI)**: Clinical syndrome of neurological deterioration due to ischemia (30% of patients)
- Not all angiographic vasospasm → DCI
- DCI can occur without significant angiographic vasospasm (microvascular dysfunction)

**Q: What other preventative measures exist for vasospasm besides nimodipine?**

A:
1. **Early aneurysm securing** - remove source of blood products
2. **CSF drainage** - remove blood from subarachnoid space
   - EVD if hydrocephalus
   - Lumbar drain in selected cases
3. **Maintain euvolaemia** - avoid hypovolaemia
4. **Magnesium sulfate** - some evidence, not routine
5. **Statins** - conflicting evidence, may have neuroprotective effect
6. **Clazosentan** (endothelin antagonist) - reduced vasospasm but not improved outcomes in trials

**Q: Why was "Triple-H" therapy modified?**

A: Evidence showed:
- **Hypervolaemia** → increased risk of pulmonary edema, cerebral edema, NO improvement in outcomes
- **Haemodilution** → reduced oxygen delivery, no proven benefit
- **Hypertension** remains the only evidence-based component
- Modern approach: "Induced hypertension with euvolaemia"

## Evidence Base

### Landmark Papers

1. **Pickard JD et al. (1989)** - BMJ 298:636-642
   - British Aneurysm Nimodipine Trial
   - Established nimodipine as standard of care

2. **Allen GS et al. (1983)** - NEJM 308:619-624
   - First major trial of nimodipine in SAH
   - Showed reduced poor outcomes

3. **Dorhout Mees SM et al. (2007)** - Stroke 38:2284-2291
   - Meta-analysis of calcium antagonists for SAH
   - Confirmed benefit of nimodipine

4. **Connolly ES et al. (2012)** - Stroke 43:1711-1737
   - AHA/ASA Guidelines for SAH management
   - Nimodipine Class I, Level A recommendation

5. **Vergouwen MD et al. (2010)** - Stroke 41:2391-2395
   - Definition of delayed cerebral ischemia
   - Standardized endpoints for SAH trials
