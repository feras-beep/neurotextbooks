# Case 02 - Glioblastoma Multiforme Management

## Case Presentation

**Clinical Scenario:**

Gentleman's histological diagnosis confirmed as **glioblastoma multiforme (WHO grade IV)**.

**Imaging:** Shows enhancing lesion with molecular structure of temozolomide

---

## Questions & Model Answers

### 1. What is the Stupp protocol? What is its evidence?

## The Stupp Protocol

**Definition:**
Combined **radiotherapy + concurrent daily temozolomide** treatment, followed by **6 further cycles of temozolomide** for glioblastoma multiforme.

### Trial Design (Stupp et al. NEJM 2005; 352: 987-996)

573 patients randomised to:

| Arm | Treatment |
|-----|-----------|
| **Radiotherapy arm** | 60 Gy total: 2 Gy × 5 days/week × 6 weeks |
| **Radiotherapy + Temozolomide arm** | Same RT + Temozolomide 75mg/m² body surface area throughout 6 weeks |

**Both arms then received:**
- 6 further cycles of temozolomide (150-200mg/m² body surface area × 5 days in each 28-day cycle)

### Results

**This paper showed:**
- **Early addition** of temozolomide to radiotherapy **statistically improves median survival**:
  - **12.1 months** → **14.6 months** (p<0.001)
- **Median progression-free survival** also improved:
  - **5 months** → **6.9 months** (p<0.001)

**Long-term Follow-up:**
- 2-year survival: 10.4% → 26.5%
- 5-year survival: 1.9% → 9.8%

### Clinical Impact

This established the **Stupp protocol** as the **standard of care for glioblastoma** in patients with:
- Good performance status
- Age typically <70 years
- Adequate bone marrow function

### 2. By what mechanism does fractionated radiotherapy work?

## Radiotherapy Mechanisms

### Basic Principle

**High energy x-rays** are accelerated towards the radiation field.

The radiation is delivered in **lower dose 'fractions'** that cause **DNA damage to cells**.

### Differential Effect

**Tumor cells** vs **Normal cells:**
- Tumor cells are **less able to repair** any initially non-lethal damage than normal cells
- Therefore **less likely to survive**

### Mechanism of Cell Death

The **unrepaired tumor cells** become **hypoxic** because of DNA damage, making them **yet more radiosensitive** to subsequent radiotherapy fractions.

**Result**: Progressive tumor cell death while sparing normal tissue

### 3. What is temozolomide? How does it work?

## Temozolomide Pharmacology

**Chemical Name:** Molecular structure shown in case (imidazotetrazine derivative)

**Classification:** Oral chemotherapeutic alkylating agent

### Mechanism of Action

1. **Depletes DNA repair enzyme**: O⁶-methylguanine-DNA methyltransferase (MGMT)
2. **Alkylating effect**: Acts as an alkylating agent, **causing methylation of MGMT promoters**
3. **Inhibits DNA repair**: Thereby **depleting MGMT stores and inhibiting DNA repair** in tumor cells

### Clinical Significance

**MGMT promoter methylation status** predicts response:
- **Methylated MGMT promoter** = Better response to temozolomide
- **Unmethylated MGMT promoter** = Poorer response

### 4. What is Gliadel®? What evidence is there for its use in GBM?

## Gliadel® Wafers

### What is Gliadel®?

**Implantable chemotherapeutic wafers** impregnated with **carmustine (BCNU)**.

**Surgical Technique:**
- Wafers are used to **line the tumor cavity** following debulking of GBM

### Mechanism

- Provides **local chemotherapy** directly to tumor bed
- Avoids systemic side effects
- High local concentration of carmustine

### Evidence Base

**Two placebo-controlled trials:**

1. **Trial 1:** Gliadel was shown to **confer improved post-operative survival** in patients undergoing initial debulking for GBM

2. **Trial 2:** Similar trial assessed **survival in patients undergoing surgery for tumor recurrence**
   - Showed **no benefit** is associated with Gliadel insertion

### Current Practice

- Used in **selected cases** at initial surgery
- **Not routinely used** in UK practice
- Considerations:
  - Cost
  - Risk of wound complications
  - Risk of chemical meningitis
  - Modest survival benefit

### 5. What is meant by the term "Performance Status"?

## Performance Status Scoring Systems

Performance status is a way to **quantify a patient's general health**.

It is used **clinically to assess** a patient's **suitability for oncological treatment** and **in research** to **approximate quality of life outcome**.

### Karnofsky Performance Status

One example of a performance status scoring system is that of **Karnofsky**, which is **a scale from 100% (normal function) to 0% (dead)**:

| Score | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **100%** | Normal, no complaints, no evidence of disease |
| **90%** | Able to carry on normal activity; minor signs/symptoms |
| **80%** | Normal activity with effort; some signs/symptoms |
| **70%** | Cares for self; unable to carry on normal activity/work |
| **60%** | Requires occasional assistance but able to care for most needs |
| **50%** | Requires considerable assistance and frequent medical care |
| **40%** | Disabled; requires special care and assistance |
| **30%** | Severely disabled; hospitalization indicated |
| **20%** | Very sick; hospitalization necessary; active support needed |
| **10%** | Moribund; fatal processes progressing rapidly |
| **0%** | Dead |

### ECOG/WHO Performance Status

Alternative widely-used scale (0-5):

| Grade | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **0** | Fully active, able to carry on all pre-disease activities |
| **1** | Restricted in physically strenuous activity but ambulatory |
| **2** | Ambulatory and capable of all self-care but unable to work |
| **3** | Capable of only limited self-care; confined to bed/chair >50% of waking hours |
| **4** | Completely disabled; cannot carry out any self-care; totally confined to bed/chair |
| **5** | Dead |

### Clinical Application in GBM

**Stupp protocol typically offered to:**
- KPS ≥70
- ECOG 0-2
- Good functional status

## Additional High-Yield Topics

### WHO Grading of Gliomas

| Grade | Type | Features | Prognosis |
|-------|------|----------|-----------|
| **I** | Pilocytic astrocytoma | Well-circumscribed, slow-growing | Excellent (often curable) |
| **II** | Diffuse astrocytoma | Infiltrative, slow-growing | Median survival 5-8 years |
| **III** | Anaplastic astrocytoma | Increased cellularity, mitoses, anaplasia | Median survival 2-3 years |
| **IV** | Glioblastoma | Necrosis, microvascular proliferation | Median survival 12-15 months |

### Molecular Markers in Glioblastoma

**Prognostic markers:**

1. **MGMT promoter methylation**
   - Better response to temozolomide
   - Improved survival

2. **IDH mutation**
   - IDH-mutant GBM have better prognosis
   - More common in younger patients
   - Secondary GBM (evolved from lower grade)

3. **1p/19q codeletion**
   - Characteristic of oligodendrogliomas
   - Predicts better response to chemotherapy

### Treatment Algorithm for GBM

```
Newly Diagnosed GBM
        ↓
Maximal Safe Resection
        ↓
Histology Confirmation (WHO Grade IV)
        ↓
    Performance Status Assessment
        ↓
   ┌────────┴────────┐
Good PS              Poor PS
(KPS ≥70)           (KPS <70)
   ↓                    ↓
Stupp Protocol:      Palliative RT
- RT 60 Gy/30#      or Best Supportive
- Concurrent TMZ     Care
- Adjuvant TMZ × 6
```

## Common Interview Questions

**Q: Why does GBM have such a poor prognosis?**

A: Multiple factors:
1. **Infiltrative nature** - impossible to achieve complete resection
2. **Highly proliferative** - rapid growth
3. **Angiogenesis** - extensive neovascularization
4. **Resistance to treatment** - heterogeneous tumor with resistant clones
5. **Location** - often in eloquent areas limiting resection
6. **Blood-brain barrier** - limits chemotherapy delivery

**Q: What are the complications of temozolomide?**

A:
- **Haematological**: Myelosuppression (thrombocytopenia, neutropenia)
- **GI**: Nausea, vomiting, constipation
- **Hepatic**: Raised LFTs
- **Infection**: Opportunistic infections (PCP pneumonia - may need prophylaxis)
- **Other**: Fatigue, headache

**Q: What imaging features suggest GBM?**

A:
- **Ring-enhancing lesion** with central necrosis
- **Irregular thick wall** with nodular enhancement
- **Extensive vasogenic edema**
- **Mass effect**
- **Crosses midline** (butterfly glioma)
- **Restricted diffusion** in cellular areas
- **Elevated CBV** on perfusion imaging

**Q: What is the role of tumor treating fields (TTFields) in GBM?**

A: TTFields (Optune device):
- Low-intensity **alternating electric fields**
- Disrupt cell division
- **EF-14 trial** showed improved survival when added to adjuvant temozolomide
- Median survival: 20.9 months vs 16 months
- **NICE approved** in UK for newly diagnosed GBM
- Patient wears device continuously
- Compliance important (>75% of time)

## Evidence Base

### Key Papers

1. **Stupp et al. (2005)** - NEJM 352:987-996
   - Established Stupp protocol as standard of care
   - RT + concurrent and adjuvant temozolomide

2. **Stupp et al. (2009)** - Lancet Oncol 10:459-466
   - 5-year follow-up of EORTC-NCIC trial
   - Long-term benefit of temozolomide confirmed

3. **Hegi et al. (2005)** - NEJM 352:997-1003
   - MGMT promoter methylation predicts benefit from temozolomide
   - Important stratification factor

4. **Westphal et al. (2003)** - Neuro-Oncology 5:79-88
   - Gliadel wafers in newly diagnosed GBM
   - Modest survival benefit

5. **Stupp et al. (2017)** - JAMA 318:2306-2316
   - EF-14 trial: TTFields + temozolomide
   - Improved progression-free and overall survival
