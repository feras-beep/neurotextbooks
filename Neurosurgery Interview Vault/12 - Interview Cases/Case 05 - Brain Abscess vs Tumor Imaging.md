# Case 05 - Brain Abscess vs Tumor - Imaging Differentiation

## Case Presentation

**Patient Context:**
Patient admitted via A&E with seizures

**Imaging:** Contrast-enhanced CT head showing large left-sided lesion with:
- Peripheral enhancement
- No pre-contrast image provided for comparison
- Left frontal lobe location
- Substantial amount of oedema
- Associated distortion of lateral ventricles

## Questions & Model Answers

### 1. Describe the contrast scan shown. What are the differential diagnoses for this lesion?

## Imaging Description

**CT Head with Contrast:**

This axial CT image of the head shows a **large, left-sided lesion** with **presumed peripheral enhancement** (no pre-contrast image is provided for comparison).

**Key Features:**
- **Location**: Left frontal lobe
- **Enhancement pattern**: Peripheral ring enhancement
- **Associated findings**:
  - Substantial amount of oedema
  - Mass effect
  - Distortion of the lateral ventricles

## Differential Diagnosis

### The Two Main Differential Diagnoses

**1. Abscess**

**2. Tumor** (high grade intrinsic or metastatic)

### Complete Differential for Ring-Enhancing Lesions

| Diagnosis | Key Features |
|-----------|--------------|
| **Abscess** | Smooth regular wall, restricted diffusion (DWI bright) |
| **High-grade glioma (GBM)** | Irregular thick wall, necrotic center, infiltrative |
| **Metastasis** | Multiple lesions common, known primary, vasogenic edema |
| **Tumefactive demyelination** | Open ring sign, incomplete enhancement, young patient |
| **Subacute infarct** | Vascular territory, gyral enhancement |
| **Resolving hematoma** | History of trauma/hemorrhage, evolving signal |
| **Toxoplasmosis** | Multiple lesions, HIV positive, basal ganglia predilection |
| **Lymphoma** | Periventricular, restricted diffusion, immunocompromised |
| **Radiation necrosis** | History of RT, difficult to distinguish from recurrence |

### 2. What are the radiological differences between high grade glioma and abscess on CT?

## Distinguishing Features on CT

### The Enhancing Rim

**Key Discriminator:**

If the **inner wall** of the enhancing rim of the lesion is **smooth and regular in appearance**, the lesion is **more likely to be abscess than tumour**.

**Abscess characteristics:**
- **Smooth, regular inner wall**
- Well-defined capsule
- Uniform wall thickness (usually 1-3mm)
- May have small, adjacent satellite lesions around periphery

**Tumor (especially GBM) characteristics:**
- **Irregular inner wall**
- Variable wall thickness
- Nodular enhancement
- Shaggy, irregular appearance

### Additional CT Features

| Feature | Abscess | High-Grade Tumor |
|---------|---------|------------------|
| **Wall thickness** | Thin, uniform (1-3mm) | Thick, irregular (variable) |
| **Wall enhancement** | Smooth, complete ring | Irregular, nodular |
| **Central contents** | Low attenuation (pus) | Necrotic, variable |
| **Satellite lesions** | May have small adjacent lesions | Less common |
| **Oedema** | Present, vasogenic | Present, often extensive |
| **Midline shift** | Proportional to size | Often marked |
| **Ventricular distortion** | Present with mass effect | Present with mass effect |

### Evidence of Infection Elsewhere

**CT may show:**
- Ventriculitis (ependymal enhancement)
- Sinusitis (source of infection)
- Mastoiditis (source of infection)
- Skull base osteomyelitis
- Meningeal enhancement

**Clinical correlation essential** for diagnosis

### 3. What further imaging would you request to exclude abscess?

## MRI for Abscess Diagnosis

**This patient should undergo urgent MRI assessment** to further assess this lesion.

### Essential Sequence: Diffusion-Weighted Imaging (DWI)

**In particular, a diffusion-weighted sequence should be included.**

### DWI Characteristics

#### In Abscess:
- **High DWI signal** is usually present centrally, implying **restricted diffusion**
- Dark on ADC (Apparent Diffusion Coefficient) map
- Reflects viscous pus with restricted movement of water molecules

#### Conversely:
- There is typically **low signal on ADC mapping**
- Confirms true restricted diffusion (not T2 shine-through)

#### In Tumor:
- **Facilitated diffusion** typically seen
- Low to intermediate DWI signal
- High ADC values
- Reflects necrotic fluid with free movement of water molecules

### Additional Helpful MRI Sequences

| Sequence | Abscess | Tumor |
|----------|---------|-------|
| **T1** | Hypointense center | Variable |
| **T1 + Gad** | Smooth ring enhancement | Irregular nodular enhancement |
| **T2** | Hyperintense center | Hyperintense |
| **FLAIR** | Hyperintense edema | Hyperintense edema |
| **DWI** | **Bright (high signal)** ⭐ | Dark to intermediate |
| **ADC** | **Dark (low signal)** ⭐ | Bright (high signal) |
| **MR Spectroscopy** | Lactate, acetate, amino acids | Choline, lipid peaks |
| **Perfusion (CBV)** | Low perfusion in wall | High perfusion in tumor |

### 4. How does this technology work?

## DWI Physics Explained

### Principle of Diffusion-Weighted Imaging

DWI allows for **comparison of the relative amounts of Brownian motion of water molecules within tissues**.

### Brownian Motion
- Random movement of water molecules
- Occurs naturally in all tissues
- Rate depends on:
  - Viscosity of environment
  - Cellular density
  - Membrane integrity
  - Temperature

### How DWI Works

**Within the thin, cystic fluid of a GBM:**
- Water molecules are **relatively free to move**
- Demonstrates **facilitated diffusion**
- **Low signal on DWI**
- **High signal on ADC**

**However, within an abscess:**
- The **thick pus** restricts movement substantially
- **Restricted diffusion**
- **High signal on DWI**
- **Low signal on ADC**

### Technical Details

**DWI uses:**
- **Diffusion-sensitizing gradients**
- Usually applied in 3 orthogonal directions
- b-values typically 0 and 1000 s/mm²

**ADC map:**
- Quantitative measurement
- Calculated from multiple b-values
- Units: mm²/s × 10⁻³
- Eliminates T2 shine-through effect

### T2 Shine-Through

**Important concept:**
- T2 hyperintense lesions can appear bright on DWI
- **NOT true restricted diffusion**
- ADC map distinguishes:
  - Restricted diffusion = low ADC
  - T2 shine-through = high ADC

### 5. What is Pott's puffy tumour?

## Pott's Puffy Tumour

### Definition

The **presence of a visible forehead swelling** due to frontal sinusitis that has caused:
- **Osteomyelitis**
- Eroded into the **subgaleal space**

### Pathophysiology

**Sequence of events:**
1. **Frontal sinusitis** (usually bacterial)
2. **Infection spreads** through bone
3. **Osteomyelitis** of frontal bone develops
4. **Erosion** through anterior table
5. **Subperiosteal or subgaleal abscess** forms
6. **Visible forehead swelling**

### Clinical Significance

**Concurrent Findings:**
- **Retrograde erosion** may also occur
- Result in the presence of a **simultaneous brain abscess**
- Both frontal bone and intracranial complications

### Epidemiology

**Risk Factors:**
- **Adolescent males** most commonly affected
- History of:
  - Frontal sinusitis
  - Recent trauma to forehead
  - Previous neurosurgery
  - Immunocompromise

### Clinical Features

**Presentation:**
- **Visible forehead swelling** (soft, fluctuant, tender)
- **Headache** (frontal)
- **Fever**
- **Purulent nasal discharge**
- Neurological symptoms if intracranial extension:
  - Seizures
  - Focal deficits
  - Altered consciousness
  - Meningism

### Imaging

**CT with contrast:**
- Frontal bone osteomyelitis
- Subgaleal collection
- May show:
  - Frontal sinusitis
  - **Epidural abscess**
  - **Subdural empyema**
  - **Brain abscess**
  - Venous sinus thrombosis

**MRI with contrast:**
- Better soft tissue definition
- Shows extent of intracranial involvement
- DWI for abscess detection

### Management

**Medical:**
- **IV antibiotics** (prolonged course 4-6 weeks)
  - Broad-spectrum initially
  - Adjust based on cultures
  - Examples:
    - Ceftriaxone 2g BD
    - + Metronidazole 500mg TDS
    - +/- Vancomycin if MRSA risk

**Surgical:**
- **Drainage of collections:**
  - Subgaleal abscess
  - Epidural abscess
  - Subdural empyema
  - Brain abscess
- **Debridement** of infected bone
- **Sinus surgery** if source

**Follow-up:**
- Prolonged antibiotic course
- Serial imaging
- Monitor for complications

## Brain Abscess - Comprehensive Overview

### Epidemiology

**Incidence:**
- 0.4-0.9 per 100,000 population
- More common in developing countries
- Male > Female (2-3:1)

**Peak Age:**
- Bimodal: Children (<10 years) and adults (40-60 years)

### Pathogenesis and Sources

**Routes of infection:**

| Route | Frequency | Sources |
|-------|-----------|---------|
| **Direct spread** | 40-50% | Sinusitis (frontal, ethmoid, sphenoid)<br>Otitis media/mastoiditis<br>Dental infections |
| **Haematogenous** | 25-35% | Endocarditis<br>Lung abscess<br>Cyanotic heart disease (R→L shunt)<br>Intra-abdominal sepsis |
| **Post-traumatic** | 10% | Penetrating trauma<br>Depressed skull fracture<br>Foreign body |
| **Post-operative** | 5-10% | Neurosurgery<br>CSF shunt infection |
| **Cryptogenic** | 10-20% | No source identified |

### Common Organisms

| Source | Organisms |
|--------|-----------|
| **Sinus/otogenic** | Streptococcus (milleri group)<br>Bacteroides<br>Prevotella<br>Peptostreptococcus |
| **Haematogenous** | Streptococcus viridans<br>Staphylococcus aureus<br>Haemophilus |
| **Trauma/post-op** | Staphylococcus aureus<br>Gram-negative bacilli |
| **Immunocompromised** | Toxoplasma<br>Nocardia<br>Fungi (Aspergillus, Candida)<br>TB |

**Often polymicrobial** (30-60% of cases)

### Clinical Presentation

**Classic Triad** (present in <50% of cases):
1. Fever
2. Headache
3. Focal neurological deficit

**Common Symptoms:**
- **Headache** (70%) - progressive, severe
- **Fever** (50%) - may be absent
- **Focal deficit** (50%) - depends on location
- **Seizures** (25-50%) - focal or generalized
- **Nausea/vomiting** (40%) - raised ICP
- **Confusion/altered consciousness** (50%)
- **Neck stiffness** (25%) - if meningitis/ependymitis

**Red Flags:**
- Rapid deterioration
- Severe headache
- Seizures
- Focal neurology
- Known risk factors

### Stages of Abscess Formation

| Stage | Time | Pathology | Imaging |
|-------|------|-----------|---------|
| **Early cerebritis** | Days 1-3 | Poorly marginated inflammation | Ill-defined hypodensity |
| **Late cerebritis** | Days 4-9 | Central necrosis begins | Better defined, some enhancement |
| **Early capsule** | Days 10-13 | Capsule forming | Ring enhancement appears |
| **Late capsule** | Day 14+ | Mature collagen capsule | Well-defined ring enhancement |

### Investigations

**Blood Tests:**
- FBC: Leukocytosis (may be normal in 30%)
- CRP/ESR: Usually elevated
- Blood cultures: Positive in 10-25%

**Imaging:**
- **CT head with contrast**: First-line
- **MRI with DWI**: Gold standard for diagnosis
- **MR spectroscopy**: Shows lactate, acetate, amino acid peaks

**Microbiology:**
- **Aspiration/excision**: Best for culture (yield 70-90%)
- **16S rRNA PCR**: If cultures negative
- **Fungal and TB cultures**: If immunocompromised

### Treatment

**Antibiotic Therapy:**

**Empirical (pre-culture):**
- **Ceftriaxone 2g IV BD**
- **+ Metronidazole 500mg IV TDS**
- **+/- Vancomycin 1g IV BD** (if post-op, MRSA risk, or severe)

**Duration:**
- Minimum **6-8 weeks**
- May need longer if immunocompromised or complex

**Surgical Management:**

**Indications for surgery:**
1. **Diagnostic uncertainty**
2. **Large abscess** (>2.5cm)
3. **Multiloculated abscess**
4. **Gas-forming organism**
5. **Fungal abscess**
6. **Failure of medical therapy** (72 hours)
7. **Ventricular rupture risk** (periventricular location)
8. **Raised ICP/herniation risk**

**Surgical Options:**
1. **Aspiration** (stereotactic or freehand):
   - Minimally invasive
   - Can be repeated
   - Provides tissue for culture
   - Decompresses mass effect

2. **Excision**:
   - Definitive treatment
   - For accessible, well-encapsulated abscesses
   - Higher morbidity
   - Better for fungal/multi-loculated

**Observation Only:**
- Small (<2cm)
- Cerebritis stage
- Good response to antibiotics
- Close monitoring essential

### Complications

**Early:**
- Herniation
- Ventriculitis
- Meningitis
- Venous sinus thrombosis
- Seizures

**Late:**
- Epilepsy (30-70%)
- Neurological deficits
- Hydrocephalus
- Recurrence (5-15%)

### Prognosis

**Mortality:** 5-20% (better with modern treatment)

**Poor Prognostic Factors:**
- GCS <13 at presentation
- Rupture into ventricle
- Multiple abscesses
- Fungal infection
- Immunocompromise
- Delayed diagnosis

## Common Interview Questions

**Q: When would you operate on a brain abscess vs treat medically?**

A: **Operate if:**
- Large (>2.5cm)
- Diagnostic uncertainty
- Multiloculated
- Failed medical therapy (>72 hours)
- Posterior fossa location
- Impending herniation
- Fungal

**Medical therapy alone if:**
- Small (<2cm)
- Cerebritis stage
- Good clinical response
- Multiple small abscesses (toxoplasmosis in HIV)

**Q: What are the complications of brain abscess aspiration?**

A:
- Hemorrhage (5-10%)
- Neurological deficit from tract
- Incomplete drainage/recurrence
- Meningitis/ventriculitis
- Seizures
- Spread of infection

**Q: How do you differentiate radiation necrosis from tumor recurrence?**

A: **Very difficult**, both can appear similar:

**Imaging clues:**
- **MR spectroscopy**: Necrosis has ↓choline, ↑lipid/lactate
- **Perfusion (CBV)**: Tumor has ↑CBV, necrosis has ↓CBV
- **PET scan**: Tumor is FDG-avid, necrosis is not
- **Biopsy**: May be necessary for definitive diagnosis
