# Case 01 - Acute Subdural Haematoma and Brain Stem Death

## Case Presentation

**Clinical Scenario:**

A 47-year-old man found unconscious in the street. On arrival in A&E:
- **GCS**: 4 (E1, V1, M2)
- **Pupils**: 6mm and sluggishly reactive to light at best
- Intubated and ventilated for CT scan

**Initial CT** (Image A): Shows acute subdural haematoma

**Post-Operative Course:**
- Day 1: Emergency craniotomy and evacuation of haematoma
- Following surgery: ICP readings remain <20 mmHg, Temperature 40°C
- GCS on sedation hold: 3

**One Week Later:**
- No change clinically
- Repeat scan performed (Images C-D)
- Pupils: Fixed and dilated since surgery

---

## Questions

### 1. What is the diagnosis? How would you manage this patient surgically?

**Diagnosis:** Left Acute subdural haematoma

**Surgical Management:**
- Patient should undergo **urgent craniotomy and evacuation of haematoma**
- If brain is anything other than 'slack', **craniectomy and ICP monitor insertion** should be considered

**Key Principles:**
- Time-critical emergency
- ABC approach first
- Early neurosurgical involvement
- Control ICP post-operatively

---

### 2. Following surgery, ICP readings remain <20 mmHg. Temperature is 40°C. GCS on sedation hold is 3. How would you proceed?

**Systematic Investigation Required:**

Investigate extensively for causes of low GCS and high temperature, other than primary insult:

1. **Post-operative imaging** to exclude:
   - Secondary insult/rebleed
   - Hydrocephalus

2. **Full septic screen** including:
   - Cardiovascular assessment
   - Endocrinological function assessment (consider hyponatraemia)

3. **EEG** to exclude seizure-related activity

**Differential Diagnosis of Post-TBI Fever:**
- Infection (meningitis, pneumonia, UTI, wound)
- Central pyrexia (hypothalamic injury)
- Seizures
- Drug reaction
- DVT/PE
- Adrenal insufficiency

---

### 3. What is "central pyrexia"?

**Definition:**
Persistent fever (>38°C) despite no other cause (sepsis) in the context of brain insult.

**Mechanism:**
- Most commonly seen in **hypothalamic compression**
- Can also occur with haemorrhagic brain insult

**Clinical Features:**
- Central pyrexia more commonly observed in **haemorrhagic** brain insult than in **ischaemic** events
- Presence of bloodied CSF may be a contributory factor

**Importance:**
- Fever is detrimental to injured brain
- Must actively cool patient
- Target normothermia (36-37°C)

---

### 4. After one week on ICU, there is no change clinically. A repeat scan is performed (images C-D). The pupils have remained fixed and dilated since surgery. What now?

**Interpretation:**
- Repeat scan shows new posterior territory and temporal infarction
- Pons/midbrain haemorrhage and surrounding ischaemia
- No change in clinical status
- Fixed dilated pupils

**Action:**
Assuming that other causes are excluded (see answer 2), it is reasonable to **consider withdrawal of care** for this patient **if brain stem death is confirmed**.

**This decision should be made by the MDT, involving the family.**

---

### 5. What does brain stem testing involve?

## Brain Stem Death Testing Criteria

### Suitability for Brain Stem Testing

**Consensus requirements:**
- **Severity of injury** - irreversible brain damage
- **Absence of effects of depressant drugs**
- **Absence of hypothermia** (all reversible causes of depressed GCS must be excluded - endocrinological, metabolic, cardiovascular)

### Brain Stem Death Confirmed If:

**Clinical Findings:**
- Pupils **fixed and dilated**
- Absent **corneal reflexes**
- No response to **supraorbital pressure**
- No **gag reflex/response** to bronchial stimulation with tracheal suction
- No respiratory effort in response to disconnection from ventilator for long enough for **pCO₂ to reach ≥6.0 kPa**

**Additional Requirements:**
- **Absent oculovestibular reflexes**: No response after each ear is irrigated with 50ml ice-cold water (ensure clear access to tympanic membranes first)

---

### 6. Who may perform brainstem testing?

**Regulatory Requirements:**

At least **two doctors** should each perform testing. Both must have been **GMC registered for ≥5 years**.

**Qualifications:**
- At least one must be a **consultant**
- The testing doctors must have experience in the field (e.g., **anaesthetists, neurosurgeons**)
- Must **not be on the transplant team**

**Timing:**
- Two sets of tests performed
- Interval between tests not strictly defined
- Usually performed several hours apart

## Key Learning Points

### Clinical Decision-Making
1. **Acute SDH** requires emergency evacuation
2. **Consider decompressive craniectomy** if brain not slack
3. **Post-operative fever** must be investigated systematically
4. **Exclude reversible causes** before considering brain death

### Brain Death Criteria
- **Preconditions**: Irreversible brain damage, no sedation, normothermic
- **Clinical tests**: Pupillary, corneal, gag, cough, apnea testing
- **Two qualified doctors** (≥5 years GMC, one consultant)
- **Not on transplant team**

### Temperature Management in TBI
- **Target**: 36-37°C (normothermia)
- **Avoid**: Hyperthermia worsens outcomes
- **Central pyrexia**: Seen with hypothalamic compression/hemorrhage

## Interview Talking Points

### If asked: "What are the challenges in diagnosing brain death?"

**Technical Challenges:**
- Excluding sedation (long half-lives of drugs in critically ill)
- Achieving normothermia
- Metabolic derangements (sodium, glucose)
- Apnea testing in patients with high baseline pCO₂

**Ethical Challenges:**
- Family communication
- Cultural/religious considerations
- Organ donation discussions
- Timing of testing

### If asked: "What's the difference between brain death and persistent vegetative state?"

| Feature | Brain Death | Persistent Vegetative State |
|---------|-------------|------------------------------|
| **Brain stem function** | Absent | Present |
| **Breathing** | Cannot breathe without ventilator | Can breathe spontaneously |
| **Prognosis** | Irreversible, legally dead | May recover in some cases |
| **Awareness** | None | None |
| **Reflexes** | Absent | Some present |
| **EEG** | Flat | May show activity |
